## Why a Private Clinic Needs a CRM and MIS

A CRM for a clinic is a system that gathers patients and requests into one funnel, automates booking and reminders, and helps bring people back for follow-up visits. Alongside it works the MIS, a medical information system, which handles doctor schedules, electronic medical records, the cash desk, and medical statistics. The first handles acquisition and retention, the second handles the treatment process and record-keeping.

Three signs show that a clinic is ready to implement a system. The front desk keeps bookings in a notebook or Excel and gets lost in reschedules. Patients come once and do not return, because no one reminds them of a follow-up visit. The director does not know which channel each patient came from or what a booking costs. If even one point describes you, manual record-keeping already costs more than automation.

## What a Clinic Loses Without an Automation System

The main hidden loss is no-shows. Without automatic reminders, the share of patients who book and do not arrive usually stays at [15–30% of bookings](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/). Every empty slot in the schedule is paid doctor time without revenue.

The second loss is quieter but larger. The clinic does not bring patients back for follow-up visits and check-ups, because there is no one to keep such a list by hand. Acquiring a new patient costs more than returning one who was already treated, so a forgotten base hits revenue harder than it seems. The third loss is blind marketing: without a CRM the director cannot see which advertising pays off and pays for channels at random.

## CRM or MIS: The Difference for a Clinic

This is the key question, and confusion about it costs money. In short: the MIS is the medical core, the CRM is the funnel and marketing. A small clinic sometimes gets by with a cloud MIS with a built-in CRM module; a clinic network more often needs a combination of an MIS and a separate CRM.

| Criterion | MIS | CRM |
|---|---|---|
| What it covers | Treatment process and record-keeping | Patient acquisition and retention |
| Core functions | Scheduling, electronic medical records, cash desk, medical statistics | Lead – booking – visit – follow-up funnel, sources, tasks |
| Integrations | LIS/RIS, national health system, private and state insurance | Telephony, call tracking, advertising, messengers |
| Main user | Doctor, front desk, accounting | Administrator, marketer, director |

If your task is to bring order to treatment records, you need an MIS first. If patients come but do not return and marketing is opaque, start with the CRM layer. At the audit we determine which of these to cover first.

## What Functions a Clinic Needs: Booking, Records, Reminders, Loyalty

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/crm-dlya-kliniki-1.svg | CRM/MIS functions for a clinic: booking, medical records, reminders, loyalty, doctor scheduling]

The function set for a clinic differs from a universal CRM. The baseline looks like this.

- **Online booking** from the website, maps, and messengers, with real-time checks of a doctor's free slots.
- **Electronic medical records** of patients with visit history, prescriptions, and documents.
- **Reminders** about a visit, follow-up, and check-up via SMS and messengers, with one-click confirmation and rescheduling.
- **A loyalty program** and tracking of repeat visits, to bring patients back instead of hunting for new ones.
- **Doctor and schedule tracking** with room and equipment load.

Reminders give the fastest effect. Reviews show they reduce no-shows by [20–40%, and combined with online booking and confirmation by up to 60%](https://doctorconnect.net/), while patients with a digital reminder are [25% less likely to miss an appointment](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/). In a summary of controlled studies, attendance with a reminder versus without is [78.6% against 67.8%, a gap of almost 11 percentage points](https://efrion.com/).

## Integrations: MIS, Labs, Telephony, Booking

A system is useful when it is connected to the rest of the clinic's environment, not standing as a separate island. We configure integrations for your profile.

- **State systems.** For licensed medical care, we set up the transfer of data to the national health system where the clinic's profile requires it.

## 152-FZ and Medical Data: What the System Must Ensure

A patient's health data is not ordinary personal data. The law classifies it as a [special category of personal data, whose processing is restricted as a general rule](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_61801/), and it is at the same time [medical confidentiality under 323-FZ](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_121895/). That is why a clinic's system faces more requirements than an online store's CRM. A working clinic system must ensure the following.

- **Consent for a special category.** A separate patient consent for processing health data, not a general checkbox.
- **Servers in Russia.** Collection and storage of Russian citizens' data [only in Russian data centers](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_61801/), including cloud MIS and CRM.
- **Role-based access and logging.** The doctor, front-desk clerk, and accountant see only their own scope, and actions on records are logged.
- **Medical confidentiality.** Technical and organizational measures against outsider access to diagnoses.
- **Data-processing agreement.** The integrator and vendor act as processors on the clinic's behalf, with responsibility fixed in writing.

The cost of a mistake has risen. A leak of a special category of personal data costs a legal entity [up to 15 million rubles in fines, a repeat leak brings a turnover-based fine of 1–3% of annual revenue, and failing to notify Roskomnadzor of a leak costs 1 to 3 million rubles](https://rtmtech.ru/). We build these requirements into the project from the start rather than patching after an inspection.

## How Implementation in a Clinic Goes

[IMG: ../assets/images/crm-dlya-kliniki-2.jpg | Stages of CRM and MIS implementation in a private clinic: audit, setup, base migration, training]

Implementation in medicine is not "switch it on and work," because the treatment process and patient data are at stake. We go through it step by step.

1. **Clinic audit.** We look at the profile, booking channels, and current records, and determine what is needed: a CRM, an MIS, or a combination.
2. **System selection.** We select a solution for the tasks and budget and justify the choice rather than pushing a single vendor.
3. **Setup.** We build the schedule, services, access roles, consent forms, and reminders.
4. **Base migration.** We carefully transfer records and patient history from notebooks, Excel, or an old system.
5. **Training.** We train the front desk and doctors to work in the system, so it takes root instead of staying empty.

## FAQ

**How does an MIS differ from a CRM, and what does a private clinic need?**
An MIS runs the treatment process: scheduling, electronic medical records, the cash desk, medical statistics. A CRM runs the acquisition and retention funnel: requests, sources, reminders, follow-up visits. A small clinic often gets by with an MIS that has a CRM module; a network usually needs a combination. What to implement first we determine at the audit based on your losses.

**Does a clinic need both systems or is one enough?**
It depends on scale and tasks. If the main pain is in treatment records, we start with an MIS that has a basic CRM module. If patients come but do not return and marketing is opaque, we add a full CRM. We do not sell extras and propose the minimum configuration that will cover your task.

**Which system to choose: Medods, Klientiks, Infoclinica, 1C:Medicine, or Bitrix24?**
There is no single correct system; the choice follows the profile and budget. Specialized MIS platforms are strong in treatment records, Bitrix24 works as a funnel and marketing layer on top of an MIS. We compare options for your clinic and justify the choice with numbers, not a preference for one vendor.

**Will a solution fit a dental or veterinary practice?**
Yes. Dental practices need a tooth chart and treatment plan, a veterinary clinic needs an animal-and-owner card, and a medical center has a broader service profile. We select a system with the right industry templates and configure it for the specific specialization.

**How does the system help reduce patient no-shows?**
Through automatic visit reminders with one-click confirmation and rescheduling. Reviews show reminders reduce no-shows by [20–40%](https://doctorconnect.net/), and attendance with a reminder versus without in studies is [78.6% against 67.8%](https://efrion.com/). The freed slots the system offers to other patients.

**Can reminders about follow-up visits and check-ups be set up?**
Yes, this is one of the most cost-effective functions. The system itself keeps a list of patients due for a follow-up, screening, and check-up and launches reminders on schedule. The clinic brings back people already treated, which is cheaper than acquiring new ones.

**Where will patient medical data be stored, and is it legal under 152-FZ?**
Health data is a [special category of personal data](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_61801/) and medical confidentiality. It must be stored on servers in Russia, with separate patient consent, role-based access, and logging. We choose a system with Russian hosting and draw up an agreement in which we act as a processor on the clinic's behalf.

**What fines does a clinic face for a leak of patient data?**
Since 2025, a leak of a special category of data costs a legal entity [up to 15 million rubles, a repeat leak brings a turnover-based fine of 1–3% of annual revenue, and failing to notify Roskomnadzor costs 1 to 3 million rubles](https://rtmtech.ru/). That is why we build data-protection requirements into the project from the start, not after an inspection.

**Can the system be integrated with a lab to transfer test results?**
Yes. We set up an exchange with the laboratory system so that referrals leave from the record and finished results return to it automatically. The front-desk clerk and doctor stop transferring forms by hand, and the patient gets results faster.

**Will you set up online booking from the website and a booking chatbot?**
Yes. We connect online booking from the website, maps, and messengers with free-slot checks. As a separate around-the-clock channel we set up a [patient booking chatbot](/uslugi/chat-boty/dlya-zapisi/), which takes requests and passes them straight into the clinic's schedule.
