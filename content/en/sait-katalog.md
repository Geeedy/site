A catalog website is a showcase of your product range with cards, filters, and search, but without a cart or online payment. The client studies products or services, compares items, learns the price on request, and instead of a "buy" button leaves a request, calls, or asks for a quote. ## What a catalog website is and why a business needs one

A catalog website is a showcase of products or services with cards, filters, and search, but without a cart or online payment. Instead of buying, the client leaves a request, calls, or asks for a price. In English-language practice this mode is called catalog mode: the site shows the range and helps you choose, while the deal itself closes offline or through a manager ([codecollab.pl](https://codecollab.pl/), [virtocommerce.com](https://virtocommerce.com/), 2026).

A showcase suits you better than a store when the final price cannot be published in advance, the range is complex, and the buying decision is made after a conversation. The business needs not an online purchase path but a convenient entry point: to show the product range, add filters and search over it, and then move the person toward a request. Such a site is cheaper and faster to build, because it has no cart, payment, or personal account.

## How a catalog website differs from an online store

The difference is not in design but in the target action. A catalog helps you choose, a store closes the deal online. If you need a cart, payment acceptance, and order processing in the system, you need an [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/), not a showcase.

| Parameter | Catalog website | Online store |
|---|---|---|
| Cart | No | Yes |
| Online payment and processing | No | Yes |
| Order checkout | No | Yes |
| Buyer personal account | Usually no | Yes |
| Target action | Request, call, price inquiry | Online purchase |
| Development timeline and price | Lower, faster | Higher, longer |

Queries with "buy," "pay," and "place an order" are store intent, and we do not pull them in here. A showcase answers a different scenario: view the range and contact the seller.

## Who a catalog website suits

A showcase without online sales suits cases where the price depends on the terms of the deal and the purchase goes through a manager ([virtocommerce.com](https://virtocommerce.com/), [codecollab.pl](https://codecollab.pl/), 2026).

- **B2B and wholesale.** The price depends on the contract, volume, and client status and is agreed individually, so publishing it as in retail does not pay off.
- **Services.** A consultation, project, estimate, installation, or rental is sold, where a request for a quote matters more than automatic payment.
- **Showrooms and complex products.** The purchase happens in the salon or at a dealer after a conversation, while the site works as an electronic showcase of the range.
- **Segments with a "price on request."** Publishing the final price does not pay off or is impossible, and a request replaces the price list.

## What catalog website development includes

A catalog is a managed data layer, not a static list: categories, SKUs, characteristics, price groups for different buyers ([virtocommerce.com](https://virtocommerce.com/), 2026). The showcase is assembled from this.

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/sait-katalog-1.svg | What a catalog website includes: cards, filters, search, comparison, request forms, product feed]

- **Product and service cards** with photos, characteristics, a description, and a price or an "on request" note.
- **Categories and navigation** across the range structure, clear to both people and the search engine.
- **Filters by properties** (size, material, brand, purpose), so the client quickly narrows the choice.
- **Catalog search** with suggestions, resilient to typos and synonyms.
- **Item comparison** for complex products where the details of characteristics decide it.
- **Request forms** in the card and in shared blocks: price inquiry, callback, quote request.
- **Product feed** from 1C or spreadsheets, so products, prices, and stock update without manual entry.

## Integration with 1C: loading products, prices, and stock

For a showcase, a one-way feed is usually enough: products, prices, and stock go from 1C to the site, and the data does not return, because there are no orders or statuses in the system. Such an exchange solves the catalog's main pain, when there are many items and updating them by hand is impossible.

By integrators' estimates, a basic one-way catalog feed costs [from 40,000 RUB and takes 1–2 weeks, while a two-way exchange with orders returning to 1C runs 120,000–250,000 RUB and 3–6 weeks](https://ds495.ru/). For a catalog website without a cart, a two-way exchange is excessive: there is simply nothing to return to 1C, so there is no need to overpay for it.

## Which CMS we build a catalog website on

We choose the engine based on catalog size, filter complexity, and the need for 1C ([hated.ru](https://hated.ru/), [no-kode.ru](https://no-kode.ru/), [topsyde.com](https://topsyde.com/), 2026). For large commercial showcases with integration we more often take Bitrix, and there is a [separate page about a Bitrix website](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/sait-na-bitrikse/) about it.

| CMS | For which catalog | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 1C-Bitrix | Large catalog, complex filters, 1C exchange | Built-in product feed, handles thousands of items |
| Tilda | Small showcase, fast start | Simple range without complex logic |
| WordPress | Flexible showcase without checkout | WooCommerce in catalog mode, without a cart |
| MODX or custom | Non-standard catalog logic | When off-the-shelf engines do not cover the requirements |

The choice of engine is not a matter of taste but a consequence of the range and integrations. At the audit we look at the number of items, filter depth, and the presence of 1C, and only then name the CMS.

## FAQ

**How does a catalog website differ from an online store?**
A catalog has no cart, online payment, or order checkout. The client chooses a product and leaves a request, calls, or asks for a price, while a manager closes the deal. An [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/) has a cart, payment processing, checkout, and a personal account, and the purchase happens online. If you need a purchase path in the system, choose a store.

**Can you show prices on a catalog website?**
Yes. Prices can be shown openly, hidden under an "on request" note, or displayed as different price groups for different buyers. A showcase without a cart does not stop you from publishing a price list, it just does not process payment online.

**How does the client place an order if there is no cart?**
Through a request. In the card and in the site's blocks there are forms: price inquiry, callback, quote request. The client chooses items and leaves contacts, and then a manager works with them. This is exactly the catalog's target action instead of an online purchase.

**Who suits a catalog website and who needs a full store?**
A catalog suits B2B and wholesale, services, showrooms, and segments with a price on request, where the deal goes through a manager. A store is needed where the client must buy and pay online themselves, without a conversation. If you are unsure, we will work through the scenario at the brief.

**How much does it cost to develop a catalog website?**
A basic catalog up to 200 items costs [from 120,000 RUB](https://dz-digital.ru/), a standard one with search and comparison [from 250,000 RUB](https://dz-digital.ru/), and a version with 1C integration [from 250,000 to 450,000 RUB and up](https://dz-digital.ru/). We lock the final price after reviewing the range and the list of features needed.

**How long does development take?**
A simple template catalog takes [3–5 weeks, a standard project with a unique design 6–10 weeks, and a catalog with 1C and complex logic 8–12 weeks](https://dz-digital.ru/). The timeline depends on the number of categories, the number of products, and filter complexity.

**Which CMS is best for a catalog website?**
For a large catalog with filters and 1C exchange we usually take a [Bitrix website](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/sait-na-bitrikse/). A small showcase we quickly assemble on Tilda, a flexible showcase without checkout on WordPress in catalog mode, and non-standard logic on MODX or a custom build. We choose the engine based on the range, not on fashion.

**How do you load products, prices, and stock from 1C?**
For a showcase we set up a one-way feed: products, prices, and stock go from 1C to the site on a schedule. A basic feed costs [from 40,000 RUB and takes 1–2 weeks](https://ds495.ru/). A two-way exchange is excessive for a catalog without a cart, because there is nothing to return to 1C.

**How many products can be placed in the catalog?**
Technically from a few dozen to tens of thousands of items. A small range suits a lightweight CMS, while a large catalog with thousands of SKUs needs an engine like Bitrix and a feed from 1C, otherwise updating prices and stock by hand is unrealistic.

**Will there be filters, search, and product comparison?**
Yes. Filters by properties, catalog search, and item comparison are the basic tools of a showcase. We tune their depth to the range: the more complex the product, the more important detailed characteristics are in filters and comparison.

**Can the catalog later grow into an online store with a cart?**
Yes. A catalog is a managed data layer, so a cart, payment, and order checkout can be added to it. If you plan online sales in the future, we will lay out the structure in advance, and more on the full functionality is in the section about the [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/).

**How does a catalog website differ from a corporate website?**
A catalog focuses on the product range: cards, categories, filters, and product requests. A [corporate website](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/korporativnyi-sait/) focuses on the company, services, and trust, and there is usually no product showcase with filters there. The choice depends on what you show the client first.

**Do you need a programmer to run the catalog after launch?**
No. The catalog is managed through the CMS admin panel: add a product, change a price, update a photo. If a feed from 1C is set up, prices and stock update automatically. We hand over instructions and, if needed, take on support ourselves.
