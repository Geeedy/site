# CRM for a retail store: one customer base, loyalty and repeat sales

In most retail stores the customer base is smeared across the register, a notebook and the salesperson's memory. Receipts are rung up anonymously, no one counts repeat sales, and campaigns go out "to everyone" or never go out at all. CRM for retail links every receipt to a customer card, shows who has not come back for a while, and launches repeat sales automatically. We are an independent integrator: we select a system to fit your store format and set it up around real processes rather than selling a boxed product. ## Why a store needs CRM

CRM for a store is a system that tracks the customer, not the product and not the receipt. Let us break down three tools that retail often confuses. An online cash register records the fact of a sale and sends the data to the tax office. Inventory software counts stock and warehouse movements. CRM stores the customer: who bought, what, how often and for how much, so you can bring them back for a repeat purchase. The register counts receipts, inventory software counts boxes, CRM counts people and their returns to the store. Full retail operations rest on all three, but it is CRM that turns one-off receipts into regular customers.

## What a store loses without CRM

Without CRM a store loses money quietly, because the losses do not show up in the revenue report. An anonymous receipt is tied to no one, so there is nothing to bring that person back with. "Dormant" customers who stopped coming do not land on any list, and they are simply forgotten. Every sale is counted as one-off, even though acquiring a new customer costs several times more than retaining a familiar one. Campaigns without segmentation fly out to everyone the same way, annoy people and burn out the base. In the end the budget goes to new traffic, while your own audience, which has already bought and trusts the store, sits idle.

## Retail CRM functions: base, loyalty, RFM, campaigns

What matters to a retail CRM is not B2B "deals" but working with the flow of customers. The key functions are these:

- **A single customer profile** covering phone, email, WhatsApp, history of receipts, returns, visits and reactions to campaigns in one card.
- **Loyalty program** with points, tiers, personal discounts and accrual rules tied to purchases.
- **RFM segments** with automatic splitting of the base by recency, frequency and amount of purchases.
- **Trigger scenarios** including a welcome for a new customer, a reminder to buy more, reactivation of the "dormant", a return after the first purchase.
- **Omnichannel campaigns** by SMS, email and WhatsApp from one window to the segment you need.
- **Repeat-sales and LTV analytics** covering average check, purchase frequency, churn and customer lifetime value.

It is the combination of "one base + trigger communications + segment analytics" that sets retail CRM apart from a simple contact list in cash-register software.

## How repeat sales and RFM segmentation work

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/crm-dlya-magazina-1.svg | RFM segmentation of store customers: Recency, Frequency, Monetary and communication scenarios]

RFM segmentation is a way to break the base into clear groups along three axes. Recency shows how long ago a person bought, Frequency how often, Monetary for how much. Each customer gets a score on these axes, and the system assembles segments from them on its own: VIP, loyal, customers at risk of leaving and "dormant". Then its own communication kicks in for each segment. A newcomer gets a welcome chain, a regular gets a personal offer for a favorite category, a "dormant" one gets reactivation with an incentive to return.

A store calculates customer lifetime value as the average check multiplied by purchase frequency and by customer lifespan ([retailcrm.ru](https://www.retailcrm.ru/), [storeradar.com](https://storeradar.com/)). Separately the system tracks the share of repeat purchases and churn. To understand the real effect of loyalty rather than an imaginary one, the measurement is run against a control group without campaigns and compared with it for the gain in repeat sales. That shows exactly what the program brought in, rather than the season or general foot traffic.

## FAQ

**How is CRM for a store different from an online cash register and inventory software?**
The register records the receipt and sends data to the tax office, inventory software counts warehouse stock, and CRM tracks the customer: who buys what and how often, and how to bring them back for a repeat purchase. These are three different tools, and in retail they work together.

**How do receipts from the register get into the customer card?**
The online cash register passes sale data under 54-FZ, and CRM matches the receipt to the customer by phone number or loyalty card. ATOL and Evotor registers integrate with retail CRM out of the box, so purchase history is collected automatically.

**What is RFM segmentation and why does retail need it?**
RFM splits the base by recency, frequency and amount of purchases and assembles segments such as VIP, loyal and "dormant". Retail needs this so as not to send everyone the same thing, but to address each group with a relevant offer and bring back those who stopped coming.

**How does CRM increase repeat sales and average check?**
The system sees who has not bought for a while and what the person took before, and launches trigger campaigns: a reminder to buy more, a personal offer, reactivation. This way the store works with an already familiar audience, and retention costs less than acquiring a new customer.

**Can a loyalty program with points and tiers be set up?**
Yes. We set up point accrual and redemption, tiers, personal discounts and rules to fit your margin. The loyalty card also links purchases to the customer card, so points work both as an incentive and as a way to recognize the customer at the register.

**Which campaigns does CRM support: SMS, email, WhatsApp?**
Retail CRM sends omnichannel campaigns from one window: SMS, email and WhatsApp. Sending goes to the RFM segment you need rather than the whole base at once, so messages reach the people who are actually interested.

**Which CRM fits a store: RetailCRM, Bitrix24 or amoCRM with MoySklad?**
It depends on the format. RetailCRM is strong in retail and online commerce, Bitrix24 gives one working loop, the amoCRM and MoySklad bundle covers the funnel plus warehouse. For a single outlet, MoySklad with CRM is enough. We select the solution at the audit, and there are pages on the specific platforms [Bitrix24](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/bitrix24/) and [amoCRM](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/amocrm/).

**Can CRM be linked to the warehouse and marketplaces?**
Yes. MoySklad or 1C provide stock, and uploads to Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market are set up through ready connectors. A package connection of all three marketplaces through MoySklad costs [from 3,500 RUB/mo](https://www.moysklad.ru/).

**How much does turnkey CRM implementation for a store cost?**
The estimate adds up from the license, the implementation work and the integrations. A RetailCRM license on "Professional" costs [10,400 RUB/mo for 3 users](https://www.retailcrm.ru/), MoySklad [from 1,595 RUB/mo](https://www.moysklad.ru/). We lock the total for the work after an audit tailored to your outlets and assortment.

**How long does implementation take?**
A project for a single retail point usually takes weeks: audit, selection, base migration, setup and training. For a chain the timeline grows because of the number of integrations and outlets, but the principle is the same, we launch in stages so the store does not stop working.

**Can the customer base be migrated from Excel, the register or an old system?**
Yes. Migrating and consolidating the base from spreadsheets, cash-register software or a previous CRM is a standard part of implementation. We clean duplicates and match contacts so purchase history is not lost in the transition.

**Will the solution fit a single store or only a chain?**
It fits both a single outlet and a small chain. For one store you take a simpler and cheaper configuration, down to free plans at the start, and for a chain you add permission separation and consolidated reports across outlets. We scale the setup to your size.
