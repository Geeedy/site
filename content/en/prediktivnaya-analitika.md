Most business decisions are made on intuition and yesterday’s reports. ## What predictive analytics is in plain terms

Predictive analytics is forecasting future events from historical data using statistics and machine learning. The model finds patterns in past sales, orders, and customer behavior, then predicts what happens next: how much will be bought, which customers will leave, when a machine will stop. Regular reporting describes the past, while predictive analytics answers the question about the future and gives you time to prepare.

## What you can forecast: demand, churn, stock, failures

Value appears where a forecast error costs money. Here are the main tasks for small and mid-sized business.

|---|---|---|

We start with one task where losses are most visible, and expand coverage after the first model has paid off.

## Forecast or answer to a question: how it differs from AI analytics

These services get confused, but their intent differs. [AI analytics](/uslugi/ii-agenty/ii-analitika/) is a conversational assistant over data: you ask in plain language “how many leads did June bring,” and it answers from current figures. It is reactive and about the present. Predictive analytics is proactive and about the future: it does not answer a question, it builds a forecast and states the probability of an event in advance.

| | AI analytics | Predictive analytics |
|---|---|---|
| Question | What has already happened | What will happen next |
| How it works | Answers a query to the data | Trains a forecasting model |
| When it helps | A fast answer on current figures | Planning purchases, retention, repairs |

If you need answers to questions about current data rather than a forecast, your tool is [AI analytics](/uslugi/ii-agenty/ii-analitika/). If you need to predict the future, you are on the right page.

## How we build forecasting models

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/prediktivnaya-analitika-1.svg | Pipeline for building a forecasting model]
[IMG: ../assets/images/prediktivnaya-analitika-1.jpg | A data specialist builds a forecasting model on company data]

A forecast is an engineering pipeline, not a single button. We go through it step by step.

1. **Data collection.** We gather sales, orders, requests history, and external factors in one place, even if they are now scattered across 1C, CRM, and Excel.
2. **Features.** We turn raw data into predictors: seasonality, promos, holidays, price, weather, customer behavior.
3. **Training.** We select a model for the task. On tabular data with seasonality, gradient boosting works well and, per market reviews, gives a forecast error of 10–15% over a 4–8 week horizon with clean history of two years or more.
4. **Validation.** We test the model on the past it has not seen (backtest) and measure the MAPE accuracy metric. If the forecast is no better than your current method, we say so honestly.
5. **Monitoring.** Demand changes, so the model is retrained on fresh data while we watch for quality drift.

## What data is needed and how much history

Predictive analytics works on what you already have. For a basic demand forecast, the following is enough:

- sales or order history by item, ideally for 6–12 months and more;
- a calendar of promos, campaigns, and price changes;
- a catalog of goods or services;
- external factors if they matter: season, weather, exchange rate, holidays.

For a churn forecast you need customer purchase and request history, for predictive maintenance you need repair logs and, where available, telemetry covering 2–3 years of failure events. The data does not have to be perfect: part of preparing for a forecast is exactly the cleaning and merging of sources. Churn and LTV forecasts come out more accurate when you have [end-to-end analytics](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/skvoznaya-analitika/) in place.

## FAQ

**How does predictive analytics differ from regular BI analytics and AI analytics?**
BI and AI analytics describe the past and answer questions about current data. Predictive analytics forecasts the future: it builds a model and states a likely event in advance. The first helps you understand what was, the second helps you prepare for what will be.

**How much historical data is needed to build a forecast?**
For a basic demand forecast, 6–12 months of sales history is usually enough, while for a stable result two years or more is better. For predictive maintenance you need 2–3 years of failure events. If there is little history, we will say so honestly at the start.

**What can you forecast in small and mid-sized business?**
Demand and sales by item, customer churn, optimal inventory level, load, and equipment failures. We start with one task where the cost of error is highest.

**How accurate are the forecasts and how is accuracy measured?**
Accuracy is measured with the MAPE metric on data the model has not seen. Per market reviews, gradient boosting gives a MAPE of 10–15% over a 4–8 week horizon with clean history of two years or more. The exact figure for you is shown by a backtest at the pilot.

**Will predictive analytics work if the data is in different systems?**
Yes. Merging sources from 1C, CRM, and Excel is a standard part of the preparation. We gather the history in one place and clean it before training the model.

**Will we need our own data scientist after implementation?**
No. We hand over a working model and set up monitoring, and we take on support and retraining ourselves, or train your team if you want to run it in-house.

**How is the model updated when demand changes?**
The model is retrained on fresh data on a schedule, and we watch for quality drift. If accuracy drops, the model is rebuilt on new patterns.

**Can you forecast customer churn and how does it help retention?**
Yes. The model flags customers with a high probability of leaving, and you work with them before they are gone. This pays off because retention is 5–25 times cheaper than acquisition.

**Does predictive maintenance work without IoT sensors?**
A basic forecast is built even from repair and runtime logs. Telemetry and sensors improve accuracy, but they are not a mandatory condition to start.

**How does your approach differ from ready-made forecasting platforms?**
A ready-made platform sells a universal model and a subscription. We build a model for your product range and processes on your data, and the cost is tied to implementation and result rather than a monthly rental.

**How long does a pilot take and what do we get as a result?**
A pilot on one task takes weeks. As a result, you get the model, measured accuracy on your history, and a calculation of the effect in money, so you can decide on production based on facts.

**What if the forecast turns out to be inaccurate?**
We check accuracy on a backtest before production. If the model does not beat your current planning method, we do not recommend implementation and will propose something that pays off. You will not have to pay for a forecast that does not work.
