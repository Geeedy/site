## What a marketplace is and how it differs from an online store

A marketplace is a multi-vendor trading platform where products are sold by several independent sellers, while the platform owner takes a commission on sales. The buyer sees a unified catalog and places a single order, even though the items in it may come from different sellers. The key difference between a marketplace and an online store is multi-vendor structure. An online store has one seller, one catalog, one logic for prices and payouts. A marketplace has many sellers, each with their own account, storefront, prices, and stock, with a unified catalog and a single checkout running on top of them.

Because of this, a marketplace is more complex in architecture: roles appear, money gets split, sellers are verified, and content is moderated. If you have one seller and one revenue stream, you need an [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/), not a platform. Below we separate all the adjacent types so you do not overpay for unnecessary complexity.

## Marketplace, online store, catalog, aggregator: what is the difference

These formats are often confused, yet their budgets and timelines differ several times over. The difference runs along two lines: who sells and where the deal closes.

| Parameter | Marketplace | Online store | Catalog | Aggregator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who sells | Many sellers | One seller | One owner | External sources |
| Where the deal happens | On the platform, unified checkout | On the store's site | No deal, storefront only | On the partner's side |
| Owner's role | Platform operator | Seller | Storefront owner | Traffic intermediary |
| Monetization | Commission, subscription, promotion | Own sales revenue | Ads, leads | Leads, ads |

A catalog shows products without an online deal and without seller accounts. An aggregator collects listings and offers without sellers hosted on the platform and without a single cart. It is exactly the multi-vendor structure with seller accounts and split payments that sets a marketplace apart from everything else. If you have a single seller, your route is the page about the [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/).

## What a marketplace consists of: the required modules

A marketplace is a set of connected modules, not a single storefront. Without any one of them the platform will not go live.

- **Buyer storefront and unified catalog** with search, filters, and product cards from different sellers.
- **Seller account**: onboarding, verification, product uploads, prices, stock, orders, and payouts.
- **Seller verification (KYC/KYB)** before they are allowed to sell.
- **Card moderation** and control of seller content.
- **Cart with split orders**: a single order is automatically divided among sellers with different delivery times, while the buyer sees one order.
- **Commission calculation and payment reconciliation** between sellers, the platform, and taxes.
- **Ratings and reviews** of sellers and products.

[IMG: ../assets/images/razrabotka-marketplace-1.jpg | Marketplace modules: seller accounts, catalog, split payments, moderation]

Seller and buyer accounts are essentially separate [web applications](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/veb-prilozheniya/) with their own logic for roles and permissions. Card moderation, description generation, anti-fraud, and recommendations we strengthen through [AI implementation](/uslugi/vnedrenie-ii/), so the operator does not check thousands of products by hand.

## Split payments and monetization models: how the platform earns

Money in a marketplace moves through the platform. The buyer pays once, and the payment gateway automatically splits the amount between the seller, the platform's commission, and taxes or fees. This is the split payment, a separate module that a regular online store simply does not have. How the split and reconciliation are set up determines both legal cleanliness and the speed of payouts to sellers.

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/razrabotka-marketplace-1.svg | Split payment scheme: how money is divided between the seller and the platform]

The platform usually earns from several models at once:

- **Commission on sales**, a percentage of each seller's order.
- **Seller subscription**, a fixed fee for being listed on the platform.
- **Paid promotion**, boosting cards and products in the catalog results.
- **Additional services** such as logistics, acquiring, advertising.

The monetization model sets the economics of the whole project, so it is calculated before development starts, not after launch.

## Development stages: from MVP to platform

A marketplace is built in iterations, not in a single launch of the full feature set at once.

[IMG: ../assets/images/razrabotka-marketplace-2.jpg | Marketplace development stages from MVP to a full platform]

1. **Analysis and model.** We calculate the economics, commissions, and roles, and pin down the niche and the seller profile.
2. **Specification and architecture.** We design the modules, split payments, data schema, and integrations.
3. **Design.** We work out the buyer storefront and the seller account as separate interfaces.
4. **MVP.** We build the catalog, cart, two roles, and one payment gateway.
5. **Pilot with sellers.** We onboard the first sellers and check onboarding, payouts, and moderation.
6. **Scaling.** We add roles, a mobile app, analytics, and integrations based on pilot data.

This order reduces risk: you pay for the next stage once the previous one has already confirmed demand.

## FAQ

**How does marketplace development differ from online store development?**
An online store is one seller, one storefront, and one revenue stream. A marketplace is a platform for many sellers with accounts, split payments, and the owner's commission. If you have a single seller, you need an [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/), not a marketplace.

**Do you make product cards for Wildberries and Ozon?**
No. We build the platform itself, we do not create a seller's cards on someone else's marketplace. Design and SEO optimization of cards on WB or Ozon is a seller's work on someone else's platform, while our service is building your own marketplace.

**How much does marketplace development cost?**
A basic MVP costs [from 800,000 to 1,400,000 RUB](https://arisweb.ru/) over [3–4 months](https://arisweb.ru/), a full platform with a mobile app costs [from 4 to 9 million RUB](https://websniper.ru/). We calculate the exact estimate after analyzing the model and the features.

**What is included in a marketplace MVP and why is it needed?**
An MVP includes a unified catalog, a cart, two roles, and one payment gateway. It is needed to test demand, seller onboarding, and the monetization model on real deals before investing in a full platform.

**How do payments and the money split between sellers work in a marketplace?**
The buyer pays once, and the payment gateway automatically splits the amount between the seller, the platform's commission, and taxes. This is a split payment, a separate module that a regular online store does not have.

**How does the platform earn: commission, subscription, promotion?**
Usually from several models at once: a percentage commission on each sale, a subscription for the seller's listing, and paid promotion of cards in the catalog. The exact set depends on the niche and is calculated at the economics stage.

**How are sellers and products verified and moderated?**
Sellers pass verification (KYC/KYB) before they are allowed to sell, and their cards go through content moderation. Bulk product checks are sped up by AI modules that filter out violations and duplicates before publication.

**What is better to build on: a ready platform or development from scratch?**
A ready platform like CS-Cart Multi-Vendor is faster and cheaper at the start for a typical marketplace. Development from scratch is justified when your model, roles, or load differ greatly from the standard. We lock the choice at the analysis stage.

**How long does marketplace development take?**
A basic MVP takes [3–4 months](https://arisweb.ru/), a full platform with an app takes [6–10 months](https://websniper.ru/), an enterprise build with integrations takes [from 12 months](https://arisweb.ru/). The timeline depends on the number of roles and the depth of integrations.

**How does a marketplace differ from a catalog site and an aggregator?**
A catalog is a product storefront without an online deal and without seller accounts. An aggregator collects listings without sellers hosted on the platform and without a single cart. A marketplace closes the deal on the platform and splits the money between sellers and the owner.

**Can we launch a marketplace and then add a mobile app?**
Yes. We recommend starting with a web MVP and adding a mobile app and extra roles after the pilot with sellers. Seller and buyer accounts we design as [web applications](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/veb-prilozheniya/) that extend to mobile platforms.

**Does the platform need support and development after launch?**
Yes. A marketplace lives on constant development: new roles, integrations, anti-fraud, seller analytics. We support the platform after launch and grow features based on data from real deals.
