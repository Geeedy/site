# CRM Audit: We Find Where the System Loses Your Money

You bought a CRM, spent months on implementation, and leads still leak, managers run deals in a notebook, and reports show something other than what is really happening. Sound familiar. By various estimates, [30% to 70% of CRM projects fail to reach their stated goals](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbestechcouncil/2019/07/22/crm-adoption-failure-rates/), and the reason is almost never the platform, but how it was set up and how it is used. A CRM audit answers one question: where exactly the system loses your money and what to do about it.

We run an independent diagnostics of an already working CRM and hand over a report with a prioritized fix plan. We do not reinstall the system and do not push a switch to the “right” platform. First we figure out what is wrong, and only then decide whether to fix or replace.

## What a CRM audit is and when you need one

A CRM audit is a structured check of how your CRM system is set up and how employees actually use it. We compare how the system should support sales with what happens in reality: how deals move through the pipeline, how clean the data is, whether integrations work, whether reports reflect reality, and whether managers follow the process. The outcome of the diagnostics is a document, not a rebuild of the system.

An audit is needed when the CRM is already in place, but it feels as if it is not there. Sales have not grown, the manager does not trust the numbers, and the sales team sees the system as a burden. In this situation there is no point ordering a repeat [turnkey CRM implementation](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/) without understanding the cause. An audit is cheaper than reinstallation and more precise than any guesswork, because it shows the specific points of loss rather than general talk about “misconfiguration.”

## Signs your CRM “is not working”

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/audit-crm-1.svg | Signs of a broken CRM and losses in numbers]

Isolated symptoms happen to everyone. It is alarming when several appear at once:

- **Managers run deals around the system.** Part of the correspondence is on a personal phone, agreements live in someone’s head, and the CRM card is filled in after the fact or not at all. According to market reviews, [companies use only a small share of CRM capabilities](https://www.nucleusresearch.com/research/single/crm-guidebook/), while the rest sits idle.
- **Leads leak between stages.** A request came in, but no one picked it up, because the source is not connected to the pipeline or no task was created. [Around 79% of leads never convert into a sale](https://www.invespcro.com/blog/lead-nurturing/), and part of these losses is directly tied to holes in the CRM.
- **Reports do not match reality.** The manager looks at the dashboard and then learns from the client about a deal that is not in the system. The data is dirty: duplicate contacts, empty required fields, outdated statuses.
- **Integrations broke or never existed.** Telephony does not log calls into the card, the website sends requests to email instead of the pipeline, 1C lives its own separate life.

If you recognized your sales team here, the problem is not fatal, but it will not resolve on its own. The diagnostics shows which of these signs you have and what they cost you.

## What we check: 6 diagnostic zones

The audit runs across six zones to cover both the technology and the people:

1. **Sales pipeline.** Whether the stages match the real deal process, whether there are “graveyard stages” where deals hang forever, whether required fields and auto-tasks are set up correctly.
2. **Data cleanliness.** The share of duplicate, empty, and outdated cards. A dirty base kills both reports and automation, so we measure it in percentages, not by eye.
3. **Access rights.** Who sees and edits what, whether the base leaks through excessive rights, and whether a role blocks a manager from actions they need.
4. **Integrations.** Website, telephony, messengers, email, 1C. We check that requests and calls actually reach the right card instead of getting lost on the way.
5. **Reports and analytics.** Whether the dashboards reflect the real picture, whether conversion is counted by stage, whether the end-to-end link from ad to money is visible, or whether that needs separate [end-to-end analytics](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/skvoznaya-analitika/).
6. **Manager discipline.** How quickly they pick up leads, whether they keep the cards updated, whether sales go around the system. Here the technology runs into the people, and this is often the main zone of loss.

## How the audit runs: stages

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/audit-crm-2.svg | Stages of running a CRM audit]
[IMG: ../assets/images/audit-crm-1.jpg | Stages of running a CRM audit]

The diagnostics takes five sequential steps and does not require a full working day from you:

1. **Access and export.** You grant guest access to the CRM and deal exports. We record what we are working with.
2. **Pipeline and data analysis.** We count conversion by stage, look for stalled deals, and measure the share of dirty cards.
3. **Interviews with managers.** Short conversations show where the system gets in the way and why people bypass it.
4. **Checking integrations and reports.** We run test requests and calls, and reconcile dashboards against the facts.
5. **Report with a fix plan.** We gather the findings, estimate the losses, and set priorities.

## What you get: a report and a fix plan

[IMG: ../assets/images/audit-crm-2.jpg | CRM audit report with a fix plan]

The result of the audit is a written report, not verbal advice. In it you will find a map of the problems found across the six zones, an estimate of what each problem costs in leads and money, and a fix plan sorted by the principle of “the cheap and painful first.”

The plan is split into three horizons. Quick fixes that pay off in days: setting up required fields, restoring an integration, cleaning obvious duplicates. Mid-size improvements: rebuilding the pipeline, automating routine, setting up reports. And strategic decisions: whether to stay on the current platform or migrate. Each item comes with an effort estimate, so you understand the scope before work starts and can do it in-house or with us.

## FAQ
**How is a CRM audit different from a repeat implementation?**
An audit is diagnostics: we find where and why the system loses money and hand over a report with a plan. Implementation is the work of reinstalling or reconfiguring. An audit is cheaper and often shows that you do not need a repeat implementation, that targeted fixes are enough.

**We use Bitrix24, do you audit that specifically?**
Yes. We diagnose Bitrix24, amoCRM, and other common systems. We check the pipeline, data cleanliness, rights, integrations, and reports. If you need improvements after the audit, [Bitrix24 setup](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/bitrix24/nastroika/) tuned to your real sales process helps.

**How long does a CRM audit take?**
Standard is 5 to 10 business days. A small system with one pipeline is checked faster, a large base with a 1C integration and several departments takes longer. We name the exact timeline after a short call, once we understand the scale of the system and the size of the base.

**What is included in the final report?**
A map of the problems across the six diagnostic zones, an estimate of losses in leads and money, and a fix plan sorted by priority. Each item of the plan comes with an effort estimate, so you understand the scope of work before the start and can do it yourself or with us.

**Do you need administrator access to our CRM?**
In most cases, guest access with read rights and deal exports is enough. Full administrator access is rarely needed and only with your consent. We record what we were given access to and do not change settings during the diagnostics.

**Will the audit stop the sales team’s work?**
No. We work with copies of the exports and guest access, and interviews with managers take 15–20 minutes each. Sales run as usual. We do not touch any settings during the audit, so as not to interfere with active deals.

**Do you check manager discipline or only the settings?**
Both. The technical setup is half the picture, the other half is how people use the system. We look at how quickly leads are picked up, whether the cards are kept updated, and whether sales go around the CRM. The main zone of loss is often right here.

**What if it turns out the CRM was chosen wrong?**
It happens, and we say so honestly, because we are not tied to a vendor. In the report we justify why the current platform does not fit and propose options. From there you can discuss [turnkey CRM implementation](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/) with base migration and preservation of deal history.

**How much does the audit cost and what affects the price?**
The price depends on the size of the base, the number of pipelines and integrations, and the volume of interviews with the team. We lock the exact estimate after the initial call and do not raise it during the work. An audit is many times cheaper than a repeat implementation and often saves its cost entirely.

**Do you fix the problems you find yourselves?**
The audit is a separate service, its result is a report. Fixes are the next step, which can be done in-house or with us. Based on the findings, we connect [sales automation](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/avtomatizaciya-prodazh/), setup, or migration. The report is written so that any competent integrator can work from it.

**How do we know it is time for an audit at all?**
If managers run deals around the system, leads leak between stages, reports do not match reality, or integrations broke, it is time. One symptom happens to everyone, but several at once is a signal that the CRM is losing money and it is worth [discussing the diagnostics](/kontakty/).

**Do you work with amoCRM and systems other than Bitrix24?**
Yes. We are an independent integrator and diagnose Bitrix24, amoCRM, and other common CRMs. The audit method is the same for any platform: six zones, interviews, an integration check, and a report. Only the specifics of a particular system’s settings differ.
