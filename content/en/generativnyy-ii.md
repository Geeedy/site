## What generative AI is in plain words

Generative AI is a technology that creates new content (text, code, images) from a natural-language request, drawing on large language models. Unlike analytical AI, which classifies data and builds forecasts, generative AI produces a result: an assistant writes a reply to a customer, a copilot puts together a contract draft, a model formulates a product description. You state the task in words, and the system returns finished text or code.

## How generative AI differs from ordinary AI

The term "artificial intelligence" covers different technologies, and the confusion costs money when you choose a solution. Analytical AI answers the question "what will happen" based on your numbers, while generative AI creates new material on request. The difference shows in the table.

| Trait | Analytical AI | Generative AI |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Classifies, forecasts | Creates new content |
| Input and output | Data turns into a label or number | A request turns into text, code, an image |
| Example task | Scoring, antifraud, [demand forecast](/uslugi/vnedrenie-ii/prediktivnaya-analitika/) | Assistant, copilot, text generation |

The two technologies often work in tandem: the forecast suggests what will happen, and the generative layer drafts an email or report about it.

## How generative AI works: models, transformers, training

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/generativnyy-ii-1.svg | How generative AI works: from prompt to a human-checked result]

At the core of the solution is a large language model (LLM) trained on huge text corpora. Most modern models rest on the transformer architecture, which captures links between words and holds the context of a request. The scheme is simple: you give a prompt and context, the transformer-based model predicts the answer word by word, and a person checks the result before it goes into action.

Answer quality depends on two things: the data the model works with and the tuning for the task. That is why the same model can be brought to a precise working tool or produce pretty but useless text. We cover quality control below, in the section on the human role and hallucinations.

## Where generative AI brings business value

Value appears where an employee spends time on repetitive work with text or search. The main uses that pay off in small and medium business:

- **We answer customers.** An assistant handles routine requests on the website and in messengers, and passes complex ones to a person.
- **We prepare documents.** A copilot drafts emails, proposals, and contracts using the company template.
- **We write and check code.** A developer gets hints and starter code right in the editor.
- **We search internal data.** An employee asks in plain language and gets an answer with a link to the document. This is a separate service, [search across internal documents (RAG)](/uslugi/vnedrenie-ii/ii-baza-znaniy/).
- **We generate marketing content.** Descriptions, posts, and newsletters in the brand's tone.

We spun off some of these scenarios into separate services because each has its own specifics. Dialogue scenarios on the website and in messengers are handled by [AI chatbots](/uslugi/chat-boty/), while multi-step tasks that call external systems are taken on by [AI agents for multi-step tasks](/uslugi/ii-agenty/). The scale of adoption is backed by research: McKinsey attributes about 75% of generative AI's potential value to four functions (customer operations, marketing and sales, software engineering, R&D) [McKinsey, The economic potential of generative AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier).

## The human role, hallucinations, and rules of use

Generative AI has a trait worth knowing before deployment. A model can produce plausible but wrong answers, and this is called hallucination. It does not "know" a fact but predicts text that looks true, so in critical scenarios a person checks the answer.

We reduce the risk at the architecture level: we limit topics, steer the model toward verified sources instead of free invention, and build rules of use into the workflows. So the team works with the tool confidently, we separately run [team training on working with AI](/uslugi/vnedrenie-ii/obuchenie-ii/): where an answer can be relied on and where a human check is needed.

## FAQ

**What is generative AI in plain words?**
It is a technology that creates new content (text, code, images) from a natural-language request. At its core are large language models trained on huge volumes of text. You state the task in words, and the system returns a finished result that a person checks before use.

**How does generative AI differ from ordinary (analytical) AI?**
Analytical AI classifies data and builds forecasts, that is, answers the question "what will happen." Generative AI creates new material on request: text, code, an image. The first works with numbers and labels, the second produces content. In real projects they often complement each other.

**What is an example of generative AI?**
An assistant that writes a reply to a customer in chat. A copilot that puts together a contract or email draft. A model that generates product descriptions in the brand's tone. A tool that suggests code to a developer. They share one thing: a request in, new finished material out.

**How does generative AI work?**
You give the model a prompt and context. A large language model on the transformer architecture predicts the answer word by word, drawing on patterns from the training data. A person checks the result. Answer quality depends on the data the model draws on and on the tuning for the specific task.

**What are transformers in generative AI?**
It is a neural network architecture at the core of most modern language models. Transformers capture links between words in text and hold the context of a request, so the model answers by meaning rather than by individual words. They are what made the current level of text generation possible.

**What is a hallucination in the context of generative AI?**
It is a plausible but wrong answer from the model. Generative AI does not store facts but predicts text that looks true, so it sometimes confidently produces an invention. The risk is reduced by limiting topics, drawing on verified data, and having a person check in critical scenarios.

**Which tasks should generative AI not solve?**
Anything where the cost of error is high and there is no one to verify the answer: legally significant decisions, final medical or financial conclusions without a specialist's control. Generative AI is good at preparing a draft and removing routine, but the final decision in responsible scenarios stays with a person.

**What is the human role when using generative AI?**
A person sets the task, checks critical answers, and makes the final decision. The model takes on routine: drafts, search, routine replies. We build in rules of use and train the team on where an AI answer can be relied on and where a mandatory check is needed.

**Which models do you use: YandexGPT, GigaChat, or open-source?**
All three, the choice depends on the task. YandexGPT and GigaChat suit cloud scenarios with data in Russia and mature Russian. Open-source models we place in the client's environment when the data is closed. We are vendor-neutral and pick a model for your requirements.

**Can data be kept inside the company's environment?**
Yes. For that we deploy an open-source model in your infrastructure, and chats, documents, or customer data never leave the perimeter. This option is chosen when data is sensitive or internal policies require it. We build the security settings in at the start of the project.

**How does this differ from a chatbot and a knowledge base?**
Generative AI is the general layer, while a chatbot and a knowledge base are its applications.
