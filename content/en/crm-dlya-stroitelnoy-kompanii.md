# CRM for a Construction Company: Sites, Estimates and Work Stages Under Control in One System

A construction business lives not on short deals but on sites that run for months. Between the first request and site handover there are dozens of touches: a visit and survey, an estimate, approval, a contract, a prepayment, work stages, subcontractors. A generic lead-focused CRM cannot hold such a cycle, and without a system requests simply get lost, stage deadlines slip, and versions of estimates and contracts diverge. We select and set up CRM around the real processes of construction and move the chaos out of spreadsheets and messengers into one system. ## Why a Construction Company Needs a CRM

A CRM for a construction company is a system that manages not an abstract deal but a site and a project with their own stages, estimates, documents and subcontractors across the whole long cycle. An ordinary CRM is built for a fast cycle of request, sale, payment, where a deal closes in days. In construction the cycle stretches over several months, with a large number of approvals, proposals and site visits, and at the center stands not a lead but a site with work stages.

Hence the difference in design. In a construction CRM the card is not just a contact but a site, to which the estimate, the contract, tasks for foremen, stage deadlines and document exchange are attached. This approach gives the manager clarity on every site and removes the situation where project data is scattered across email, Excel and employees' heads.

## What a Construction Company Loses Without a CRM

While sites are run in spreadsheets and chat, losses go unnoticed one by one, but together they eat into the margin. A request from a trade show or the website is forgotten because there was no one to pick it up. A work stage slips its deadline, and the manager finds out after the fact. The estimate was recalculated, but the old version went into the contract. There is no single picture of the site, and at the planning meeting everyone quotes their own figures.

A construction CRM closes exactly these gaps. It records every incoming request and does not let it get lost, keeps stage deadlines under control, stores estimates and contracts with versions in the site card, and shows the manager the status of all sites in one window. Descriptions of industry rollouts show that companies automate the [pipeline from request to site handover, tracking of sites and lots, control of work stages, estimate approvals and document flow](https://okocrm.com/blog/crm-dlya-stroitelnoj-kompanii/) as a single loop rather than a set of disconnected tools.

## CRM Features for Construction: Sites, Projects, Estimates, Documents

The industry value of a CRM in construction is assembled from several blocks that work around the site.

- **Site and project card.** A single place that holds the client, address, estimate, contract, deadlines and the whole history of the site.
- **Work stages and deadline control.** The project is split into stages, and for each you see the status, the owner and the deadline.
- **Estimates and proposals.** Calculations and proposals are created and stored in the card, with versions and approval statuses.
- **Document flow.** Contracts, annexes and acts sit in one place, without lost versions and searching through email.
- **Tasks for foremen and subcontractors.** Work is set as tasks with owners and deadlines, and the manager sees the execution.
- **Working with subcontractors.** External crews and subcontracting are built into the same loop of tasks and approvals.
- **1C exchange.** Invoices, acts and settlements sync with accounting instead of being entered twice by hand.

## How the Deal Pipeline Works in Construction

The main difference between construction and classic sales is the length and structure of the pipeline. The deal goes through not three or four but nine steps, and each can take weeks.

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/crm-dlya-stroitelnoy-kompanii-1.svg | Deal pipeline in construction: from request to site handover (9 stages of the long cycle)]

A typical construction company pipeline looks like this: [incoming request, qualification, visit and survey, proposal and estimate, estimate approval, contract, prepayment, construction stages, site handover](https://spark.ru/startup/263392). The CRM moves the client through these stages, prompts the next step and does not let the deal stall between the survey and the estimate or between approval and the contract. At any moment the manager sees how many sites are at each stage, where money is already in play, and where a deal is stuck and needs attention.

## 1C Integration and Document Flow

For construction the CRM to 1C link is almost mandatory. Invoices, acts, settlements and source documents live in accounting, and without data exchange managers and accountants duplicate the same thing by hand, with errors and discrepancies. [Bitrix24 states deep integration with 1C through ready modules or an API](https://helpdesk.bitrix24.ru/open/14125856/), so invoices and acts for a site can be handled without double entry.

The second layer is document flow. Estimates, contracts and annexes are tied to the site card rather than scattered across folders and email. Versions are not lost, approval runs inside the system, and the change history is visible. Together, accounting and sales work with the same data, and the manager gets consistent figures for every site.

## FAQ

**How does a CRM for a construction company differ from a generic CRM?**
A generic CRM runs a short deal of request, sale, payment. A construction one runs a site and a project with work stages, estimates, contracts and subcontractors across a long cycle of several months. At the center stands not a lead but a site, to which everything else is attached.

**Will Bitrix24 or amoCRM work for construction, or do you need an industry solution?**
For most construction companies a generic platform with industry setup is enough. Bitrix24 is more flexible and holds a single loop with 1C and sites better; amoCRM is simpler and suits small teams with a sales focus. An industry solution is needed where processes are very specific. Which to choose for your tasks, we determine at the audit.

**How do you track sites, projects and work stages in a CRM, not just deals?**
We set up the site card as the main entity: to it are attached work stages with deadlines and owners, the estimate, the contract and tasks. The manager sees the status of every site and every stage, not just the sales stage.

**Can you keep estimates, proposals and contract versions in the CRM?**
Yes. Estimates and proposals are created and stored in the site card with versions and approval statuses. Contracts and annexes sit there too, so old versions do not go into work by mistake.

**Can integration with 1C on invoices, acts and settlements be set up?**
Yes, for construction this is an almost mandatory block. Bitrix24 integrates with 1C through ready modules or an API; invoices, acts and settlements sync without double entry. The specific exchange scenario we set up around your accounting scheme.

**How does a CRM help control a long deal cycle of several months?**
The system moves the client through all nine pipeline stages from request to site handover, prompts the next step and highlights stalled deals. At any moment the manager sees how many sites are at each stage and where a deal is stuck.

**Can you assign tasks to foremen and subcontractors and control stage deadlines?**
Yes. Work is set as tasks with owners and deadlines, including external crews and subcontracting. The foreman sees their tasks, and the manager sees the execution and stage deadlines for every site.
