## Why small business chooses a store on Tilda

A store on Tilda is a small online store on the no-code platform Tilda with a built-in cart and online payment acceptance, built without programming on a visual builder. It differs from a [regular online store on a CMS](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/): there the platform is heavier and more flexible, here speed and simplicity matter more than depth. For a small business's first store this is a deliberate choice in favor of a fast start.

Three reasons small business goes specifically with Tilda:

- **Speed of launch.** The store is assembled from ready-made blocks, so sales open in weeks, not months.
- **No-code.** You manage the catalog, cards, and cart yourself through the visual editor, without a developer for every edit.
- **Cost of entry.** A subscription platform instead of expensive CMS development, and the turnkey estimate is lower than for heavy solutions.

It is important not to confuse a store on Tilda with a [site on Tilda](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/sait-na-tilde/) in general. A site on Tilda is landing pages, business cards, and multi-page sites without sales, while here we mean exactly a store with a cart, payment, and product catalog.

## Built-in cart and payment acceptance on Tilda

The cart on Tilda is built in: the buyer adds one or several products and places the order right on the site. By default payment methods are not connected; they are added separately in the "Site Settings → Payment Systems" section ([help.tilda.cc/online-store/payment-systems](https://help.tilda.cc/online-store/payment-systems)). If one system is connected, after checkout the buyer goes to its payment page, and if there are two or more systems, a payment method choice appears in the cart.

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/magazin-na-tilde-1.svg | How an order works in a Tilda store: cart, payment choice, product reservation]

This is how an order proceeds in a store on Tilda:

1. **Product to cart.** The buyer picks an item from the catalog and adds it to the cart.
2. **Checkout.** In the cart they enter contacts and delivery details.
3. **Payment choice.** With several connected systems the buyer selects a convenient method.
4. **Payment.** The buyer goes to the payment system page and pays for the order.
5. **Reservation.** The paid product is reserved, and the number of available units decreases.

Tilda integrates natively with popular payment providers. In Russian practice these are YooKassa, Robokassa, Tinkoff, and others, while the help center mentions Stripe, PayPal, and Verifone; there is a pay-on-delivery option and promo codes ([help.tilda.cc](https://help.tilda.cc/online-store/payment-systems)). When payment starts, the product is reserved in the catalog, and auto-release of the reservation is configurable in a range from 15 minutes to 7 days ([help.tilda.cc](https://help.tilda.cc/online-store)).

## Catalog and products: how much Tilda handles

Tilda's catalog comfortably covers small-business needs. Limits depend on the plan, and even the entry plan gives a large headroom in the number of positions.

| Parameter | Personal | Business |
|---|---|---|
| Products in catalog | up to [5000](https://help.tilda.cc/online-store/catalog) | up to [50,000](https://help.tilda.cc/online-store/catalog) |
| Categories | up to 1000 | up to 1000 |
| What the plan is taken for | landing, catalog, simple store | full store, API, CRM |

For a store you generally take the Business plan: it is designed for store scenarios, cart, code export, API, and receiving requests into a CRM. Personal is more often used for a landing page, business card, or simple catalog ([tilda.cc/ru/pricing](https://tilda.cc/ru/pricing)). For small business the limits are more than enough, so we focus not on catalog volume but on the convenience of cards, categories, and the cart.

## Whom Tilda fits and whom it does not

We say this honestly up front, so you do not pay for a platform that will not suit you. Tilda is a no-code builder with a store, not a full CMS at the level of Bitrix or Shopify. E-commerce functions exist but are shallow: a basic catalog, cart, checkout, and minimal stock tracking ([ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/tilda-review/)).

Tilda fits if you have:

- a small assortment and a first or second store;
- a need for a fast selling start and a limited budget;
- no exchange with 1C and no real-time inventory tracking;
- a wish to run the catalog yourself, without a developer.

Tilda will be too small if you need a heavy catalog with deep filtering, exchange with 1C, thousands of SKUs, and high load. That is already a [store on Bitrix](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/na-bitrikse/), a larger and heavier solution.

## SEO of an online store on Tilda

A store on Tilda can and should be prepared for search. We set human-readable page addresses, write unique titles and descriptions per category and card, build a sitemap, and watch loading speed. Tilda lets you manage these settings, so the store reaches search no worse than on a CMS, provided the basic optimization is done carefully.

Separately, we connect your own domain and remove the builder mention, so the store looks like an independent brand rather than a template site. This affects both buyer trust and how search engines perceive the store.

## When you outgrow Tilda

Tilda is designed for a small, fast store, and that is its strength, not a flaw. But business grows, and at some point the platform becomes too small. Signs that it is time to think about moving:

- the catalog has grown to thousands of positions and become hard to manage;
- you need exchange with 1C and real-time inventory;
- complex filtering and personal pricing have appeared;
- load has grown, and performance and flexibility now matter more.

In that case we move the store to a more powerful platform without losing sales. That is already an [online store on Bitrix](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/na-bitrikse/): a large catalog, integration with 1C, thousands of SKUs, and high load. Tilda remains the fast start, and Bitrix becomes the next step once the store has genuinely grown.

## FAQ

Yes, for small business it is. Tilda gives a built-in cart, online payment acceptance, and a catalog with categories, which is enough for a small store. What makes it full is not the platform's size but a working combination of catalog plus cart plus payment, and all of that assembles on Tilda.

Payment methods connect in the site settings. In Russian practice these are YooKassa, Robokassa, Tinkoff, and other providers; there is a pay-on-delivery option and promo codes. If you connect several systems, the buyer selects a convenient method right in the cart.

**How many products will a store on Tilda hold?**
The catalog handles [up to 5000 products on the Personal plan and up to 50,000 on Business](https://help.tilda.cc/online-store/catalog), with up to 1000 categories on both plans. For small business these limits are more than enough, so you will not run into the catalog ceiling.

For a full store you take Business: it is designed for store scenarios, cart, API, and receiving requests into a CRM. Personal more often fits a landing page, business card, or simple catalog. We will suggest the plan for your task at the start.

**How is a store on Tilda different from a regular online store on a CMS?**
On a CMS the store is heavier, more flexible, and designed for a large catalog and complex integrations. On Tilda it is simpler, faster, and cheaper to start, but with simpler logic. We cover the full range of options on the [online store](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/internet-magazin/) page.
