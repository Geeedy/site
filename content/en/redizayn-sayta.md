## What a website redesign is

A website redesign is an update of the design, UX, responsiveness and structure of an already existing site while keeping the content and the accumulated SEO rankings. We work with what you already have instead of starting on a blank page. The visuals become more modern, the paths to a lead shorter, the mobile version more convenient, while the texts, pages and their addresses stay the same wherever possible. This is what sets a redesign apart from building a new site, where everything is designed anew from research to markup, and the search weight built up over years is reset along with the old domain or structure.

## When a site needs a redesign: the signs

A redesign pays off when the site gets in the way of sales, not just when it looks old-fashioned. Below are the signals that it is time for a rework.

[IMG: ../assets/images/redizayn-sayta-1.jpg | Signs that a site needs a redesign: dated design, low conversion, no responsiveness]

- **Dated visuals.** The design looks older than competitors and lowers trust in the first seconds.
- **Poor UX.** Long paths to a lead, unclear buttons, heavy forms, the visitor gets lost.
- **Low conversion.** Below 2-3% for B2C and below 1% for B2B is a reason to look into the site.
- **No responsiveness.** On a phone the layout breaks or is awkward, and mobile traffic leaves.
- **Product or positioning change.** The range, audience or offer changed, but the site stayed the same.

According to [lpmotor.ru](https://lpmotor.ru/), sites with modern design show 32% higher engagement, so an update pays off beyond the visuals alone.

## What we change in a redesign: design, UX, responsiveness, structure

A redesign can be targeted or deep, but we work across four areas.

- **Design.** Visual style, design system, typography, colors, a consistent look across all pages.
- **UX.** User journeys, navigation, placement and logic of CTAs, simpler forms.
- **Responsiveness.** Mobile and tablet versions, speed and comfort on small screens.
- **Structure.** Page map, landing pages, block order, so the important things sit closer to the lead.

You can update everything at once, or only the responsive layout or the home page. If the outcome calls for a full [corporate website](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/korporativnyi-sait/) as a separate type rather than a rework of the current one, we will say so honestly at the start.

## How we keep SEO rankings during a redesign

This is the key block and our main area of responsibility. Ranking loss after a rework does not come from the new design, but from careless work with addresses and metadata. We close these risks with a safe-release process.

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/redizayn-sayta-1.svg | How to keep SEO rankings during a redesign: redirect map, URL preservation, metrics before and after]

The main rule is simple: do not change URLs without need. If a page ranks well, its address stays the same. When the structure does change, we set a one-to-one 301 redirect from each old URL to a page with the same meaning and intent, not to the home page "just in case." That is how search engines carry the accumulated weight to the new address, according to [antonvetrov.ru](https://antonvetrov.ru/) and [seotika.ru](https://seotika.ru/).

- **Redirect map.** A document in the format "old URL – new URL – 301 type," where every old address has a match. Only 301, because 302 does not pass link weight, and every rule is checked by hand, per [seo-smirnov.ru](https://seo-smirnov.ru/).
- **Baseline metrics before the start.** We record keyword rankings, traffic, conversion, the list of top donor pages and all indexable URLs with their meta tags. This is the reference point, per [ranx.ru](https://ranx.ru/) and [onepix.ru](https://onepix.ru/).
- **Safe release.** We build the new site on a test domain closed from indexing. After the move we update sitemap.xml and robots.txt, check indexing and watch behavioral metrics.

The reference point is best fixed in advance and independently, so before a deep redesign we recommend an [SEO audit](/uslugi/seo-prodvizhenie/seo-audit/) of the current site, to have a snapshot of rankings and entry points before any edits.

## Redesign, site migration and a new site: the difference

These three jobs are often confused, yet they are paid for differently. The table sorts them by meaning.

| Parameter | Redesign | Site migration | New site |
|---|---|---|---|
| What we do | Change design, UX, responsiveness, structure | Move the site "as is" to another host or CMS | Design and build from scratch |
| What about URLs | Keep where possible, otherwise one-to-one 301 | Keep them, set redirects when the CMS changes | Create a new address structure |
| What about content | Keep it, adapt to the new structure | Move it unchanged | Write it anew |
| When to choose | The site is dated or converts poorly | You need a move to a new host or engine | There is no site or it is unusable |

If what you need is precisely a move to another host or CMS without a visual rework, your task is a [site migration](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/perenos-sayta/), a separate service. Redesign and migration often go together, and then we combine them in one release with a single redirect map.

## Redesign stages

We run the work step by step, with SEO control built into every stage rather than added at the end.

[IMG: ../assets/images/redizayn-sayta-2.jpg | Website redesign stages from audit to release with SEO preserved]

1. **Audit.** We examine the current site, measure rankings, traffic and conversion, and find weak UX spots.
2. **URL map.** We compile a list of all indexable addresses and decide what stays, what changes, what gets redirected.
3. **Prototype.** We design the page structure and user journeys before any drawing, and agree the logic with you.
4. **Design.** We draw the mockups in a single design system and show them on desktop and mobile.
5. **Markup.** We build responsive pages on a test domain closed from indexing.
6. **Release checklist.** We roll out, verify the 301 redirects, update sitemap.xml and robots.txt, and monitor indexing and rankings.

- **Not ongoing support.** A redesign is a one-off project, while regular small tweaks are separate maintenance.

## FAQ

**How much does a website redesign cost?**
It depends on the site type and the depth of rework. Reference points: a landing page 30,000 – 60,000 RUB, a corporate website 60,000 – 150,000 RUB, an online store from 100,000 RUB. A cosmetic visual refresh is cheaper, a deep UX rebuild is more expensive. We name the exact estimate after a short audit.

**Will I lose rankings in Yandex and Google after a redesign?**
No, if you keep the URLs, set one-to-one 301 redirects and do not lose content. This is exactly our area of responsibility: we measure rankings before the start, maintain a redirect map and compare metrics after release, so a drop is spotted right away rather than a month later.

**How does a redesign differ from building a new site?**
A redesign reworks an existing site and keeps the content and the accumulated rankings. A new site is built from scratch from research to markup, and the search weight has to be earned all over again. If the site works overall, a redesign is more cost-effective.

**How does a redesign differ from a site migration?**
A migration is a move to another host or CMS "as is," without a visual rework. A redesign is a rework of the design and UX while keeping the platform. If what you need is precisely a move, see [site migration](/uslugi/razrabotka-saitov/perenos-sayta/). These jobs are often combined in one release.

**How do I know it is time for a redesign?**
Dated looks, poor UX, low conversion, a broken mobile version or a product change. If at least one point is about you and the site gets in the way of sales, a redesign will pay off. An audit of current metrics gives the exact answer.

**How long does a redesign take?**
A landing page 1-3 weeks, a corporate website 3-6 weeks, an online store 1.5-3 months. With careful UX work the timeline grows, and large projects with a structure rebuild take 3-5 months. We name the exact timeline after a scope audit.

**Do page addresses change during a redesign?**
We try not to change them. If a page ranks well, its URL stays the same. When the structure does change, we set a 301 redirect from each old address to a page with the same meaning, so the weight passes to the new URL.

**Will my text and content be kept?**
Yes, we carry the content over. When needed we refine the texts for the new structure and landing pages, but we do not discard what already attracts traffic. Your content is an asset, and we protect it.

**Can you redesign only the mobile version or only the home page?**
Yes, a redesign can be targeted. You can update only the responsive layout, only the home page or a single section without touching the rest. This is cheaper than a full rework and suits cases where the problem is local.

**How do you check that traffic did not drop after a redesign?**
We measure rankings, traffic and conversion before the start and compare them after release. We monitor the indexing of new addresses and behavioral metrics. If something dips, we see it right away and fix it instead of waiting for complaints.

**Do you use AI in a redesign?**
Yes. AI speeds up prototyping, mockup generation and UX hypothesis testing. Because of this the work goes faster and the cost for the client is lower than with a fully manual approach. The final decisions are still made by a designer.

**Is support needed after a redesign?**
Ongoing maintenance is a separate service, not part of a one-off redesign project. After release the site is fully operational. If you need constant tweaks and monitoring, we will discuss maintenance separately.
