# CRM for a Law Firm: Matters, Deadlines, Documents and Billing in One System

A law firm does not live on abstract "deals" but on matters, clients, and deadlines you cannot miss. As long as matters live in Excel, a calendar, and folders on someone's drive, inquiries get lost, hearings are forgotten, and billable hours go unrecorded. CRM for law firms pulls this into one system, where every client is linked to their matter, every deadline to a responsible lawyer, and every document to a matter card. We select and set up such a system for how your practice actually works. This is part of our approach to [CRM implementation](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/): first a process audit, then a setup built around how the firm really operates.

## Why a law firm needs a CRM

CRM for law firms is a specialized system that helps a practice manage clients, track matters, control deadlines, and automate document handling. What sets it apart from an ordinary CRM is one key connection: client, matter, deadline and hearing, document, and a lawyer's billable hour, instead of a single abstract "deal." A single database holds the full history of inquiries and correspondence, tasks are tied to procedural deadlines, and routine work, from drafting contracts to deadline reminders, moves into automation. The managing partner stops holding the firm's workload in their head and sees it in a report.

## What a law firm loses without a CRM

When matters are scattered across different places, losses build up quietly and add up to money. Here is what slips away most often.

- **Procedural deadlines and hearings.** A date is logged in one calendar while the responsible lawyer looks at another, and the hearing surfaces at the last moment.
- **First inquiries.** A call or email never becomes a task, and a prospective client goes to whoever called back faster.
- **Billable hours.** Consultations and document preparation go unrecorded, the client is invoiced from memory, and the bill ends up smaller than the actual work.
- **Matter documents.** The current version of a contract sits on someone's laptop instead of in the matter card, available to the whole team.

CRM closes each of these gaps with a separate mechanism, and we break them down below.

## CRM features for law firms: matters, deadlines, documents, billing

The value of the system for this industry is that it is built around a lawyer's work, not a sales manager's. The core feature set looks like this.

- **Matter and client tracking.** A matter card with full history, parties, status, and linked documents, plus a single client database with no duplicates.
- **Control of procedural deadlines and hearings.** A matter calendar, reminders to the responsible lawyer and a backup, and automatic recalculation of deadlines by rule.
- **Document templates and generation.** Contracts, powers of attorney, warrants, and claims are assembled from card data, with versioning and storage in the matter.
- **Time tracking and billing.** Time is logged by matter and lawyer, which feeds the client invoice and a picture of the team's workload.
- **Intake pipeline and access rights.** The path from inquiry to engagement is governed by statuses, with separated rights and confidentiality of client data.

One important boundary: the CRM stores documents and assembles them from templates, but the actual review of a contract for risks and against checklists is handled not by the CRM but by an [AI agent for contract review](/uslugi/ii-agenty/dlya-yuristov/). The CRM manages the matter, the AI agent reads and assesses the document by meaning. These are different tools, and it is good when they stand side by side.

## How the pipeline from inquiry to engagement works

Before a matter exists, there is an inquiry that is easy to lose. The intake pipeline in the CRM moves it through stages and keeps it from going cold.

[INFOGRAPHIC: ../assets/infographics/crm-dlya-yuristov-1.svg | Law firm pipeline: from first inquiry to signed engagement]

A law firm client's path usually looks like this: first inquiry, qualification, consultation, proposal and fee estimate, engagement, opening the matter. A lead is created automatically from a website form, a call, or an email, and statuses and reminders keep the inquiry from stalling without a reply. In Western practice these functions are split across two systems: an intake CRM runs the pipeline up to signing, and practice management runs the matter after it. In Russian firms both tasks are more often handled by one configured system, and that is more convenient, because the client and their matter live in one place from the first call to closing.

## Which CRM suits a law firm

The choice usually comes down to a dedicated legal system versus a universal platform configured for the practice. Each path has its own logic.

| Criterion | Dedicated legal CRM | Universal (Bitrix24) |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | XSUD, Yurayt24, MegaCRM | Bitrix24 for legal practice |
| Court matter tracking | Out of the box, built for the process | Configured for the firm |
| Flexibility and integrations | Narrower, focused on legal specifics | Broader: telephony, email, tasks |
| When to choose | Heavy court work and enforcement proceedings | You need a pipeline, integrations, and growth across the whole firm |

Dedicated systems such as XSUD track court matters and enforcement proceedings, Yurayt24 automates court representation and the financial accounting of legal practices, and MegaCRM covers client work. The universal path is offered by [Bitrix24 for legal practice](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/bitrix24/) and Megaplan with deep automation of case handling. If the firm is small, it is worth looking at solutions [for small business](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/crm-dlya-malogo-biznesa/) too, where simplicity and price matter more than a court module.

## Integrations: telephony, email, documents, calendar

A CRM pays off when it stops being a separate window and connects to the firm's working tools.

[IMG: ../assets/images/crm-dlya-yuristov-1.jpg | Integration of a law firm CRM with telephony, email, document storage, and a court hearings calendar]

- **Telephony.** On an incoming call the client card pops up, the conversation is recorded and filed to the matter and the responsible lawyer.
- **Email.** Two-way sync with Outlook, Microsoft 365, or Gmail: correspondence on a matter is saved in its card, not in a personal inbox.
- **Documents.** A link to storage and templates, auto-generation of contracts and claims from CRM data, and versioning.
- **Calendar.** Court dates sync with the working calendar, with reminders about hearings and procedural deadlines to the responsible lawyer and a backup.

## FAQ

**How is CRM for law firms different from an ordinary CRM?**
An ordinary CRM runs abstract deals and sales. CRM for law firms runs the connection of client, matter, deadline, document, and billable hour: it is built for matter tracking, control of procedural deadlines, document storage, and hourly billing, not for a pipeline selling a product.

**Will Bitrix24 fit a law firm, or do you need a dedicated legal CRM?**
It depends on the volume of court work. If there is a lot of it and enforcement-proceedings tracking matters, dedicated systems such as XSUD or Yurayt24 are closer. If you need a pipeline, integrations, and growth across the whole firm, Bitrix24 configured for legal practice is more often the choice. In the audit we work through your case and give an honest recommendation.

**How does the CRM control procedural deadlines and remind about court hearings?**
Deadlines and hearing dates live in the matter card and sync with the working calendar. The system sends reminders to the responsible lawyer and a backup in advance, and strong configurations help calculate deadlines by rule rather than keeping them in your head.

**Can matter documents be stored and claims, contracts, and powers of attorney generated from templates?**
Yes. Documents are stored in the matter card with versioning, and contracts, powers of attorney, warrants, and claims are assembled from CRM data using templates. This speeds up preparation and removes inconsistency in wording.

**Does the CRM review a contract for risks?**
No, and this is an important distinction. The CRM stores and generates documents, but analysis of a contract against checklists and for risks is handled by an [AI agent for lawyers](/uslugi/ii-agenty/dlya-yuristov/). The pairing is simple: the CRM runs the matter, the AI agent reads and reviews the document. We can implement both tools.

**How does billing and time tracking for lawyers work in the CRM?**
A lawyer logs time by matter, and from these records the client invoice and a picture of the team's workload are formed. The partner sees how many hours went into a matter and who is busy with what, and unbilled consultations stop getting lost.

**Can the CRM run a pipeline from first inquiry to signed engagement?**
Yes. An inquiry automatically becomes a lead from a website form, a call, or an email, and moves through stages: qualification, consultation, fee estimate, engagement, opening the matter. Statuses and reminders keep the inquiry from stalling without a reply.

**Can telephony and email be integrated so calls and emails are filed to the matter?**
Yes. On an incoming call the client card pops up, the conversation is recorded and linked to the matter. Email syncs with Outlook, Microsoft 365, or Gmail, and correspondence on a matter settles in its card rather than in the lawyer's personal inbox.

**How are client data confidentiality and access rights handled?**
Rights are configured by role: a lawyer sees their own matters, a practice head sees theirs, a partner sees the full picture. Access to sensitive matters is limited, actions are logged, and data stays in your environment.

**How much does turnkey CRM implementation for a law firm cost?**
The estimate has two parts: the platform license and the implementation work. The license is set by the chosen system, and we lock the setup cost after a short process audit, because the scope for a solo lawyer and for a firm with a litigation department is different.

**Will the solution suit a solo practitioner or attorney, not only a large firm?**
Yes. For a single lawyer a basic configuration is set up: matter card, deadline calendar, and a simple pipeline. A small firm may also fit solutions [for small business](/uslugi/vnedrenie-crm/crm-dlya-malogo-biznesa/), where simplicity and price matter more than a heavy court module.

**Are you tied to one CRM, or do you select for our processes?**
We are an independent integrator and are not bound to a single vendor. First we look at how the firm works, then we recommend a system that fits the processes, and set it up. If a dedicated CRM fits better than a universal one, we will say so.
