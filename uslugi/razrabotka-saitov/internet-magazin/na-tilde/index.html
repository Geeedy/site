Turnkey Online Store on Tilda

Tilda solves a common small-business task: launch a first online store quickly and without a heavy CMS. Here we build exactly a small online store on Tilda with a built-in cart, online payment acceptance, and a catalog. This format fits when the assortment is small, the budget is limited, and sales need to open in the coming weeks rather than in six months. It is one of the directions in our website development, and on this page we honestly outline what Tilda handles and where its ceiling begins.

Why small business chooses a store on Tilda

A store on Tilda is a small online store on the no-code platform Tilda with a built-in cart and online payment acceptance, built without programming on a visual builder. It differs from a regular online store on a CMS: there the platform is heavier and more flexible, here speed and simplicity matter more than depth. For a small business's first store this is a deliberate choice in favor of a fast start.

Three reasons small business goes specifically with Tilda:

  • Speed of launch. The store is assembled from ready-made blocks, so sales open in weeks, not months.
  • No-code. You manage the catalog, cards, and cart yourself through the visual editor, without a developer for every edit.
  • Cost of entry. A subscription platform instead of expensive CMS development, and the turnkey estimate is lower than for heavy solutions.

It is important not to confuse a store on Tilda with a site on Tilda in general. A site on Tilda is landing pages, business cards, and multi-page sites without sales, while here we mean exactly a store with a cart, payment, and product catalog.

Built-in cart and payment acceptance on Tilda

The cart on Tilda is built in: the buyer adds one or several products and places the order right on the site. By default payment methods are not connected; they are added separately in the "Site Settings → Payment Systems" section (help.tilda.cc/online-store/payment-systems). If one system is connected, after checkout the buyer goes to its payment page, and if there are two or more systems, a payment method choice appears in the cart.

This is how an order proceeds in a store on Tilda:

  1. Product to cart. The buyer picks an item from the catalog and adds it to the cart.
  2. Checkout. In the cart they enter contacts and delivery details.
  3. Payment choice. With several connected systems the buyer selects a convenient method.
  4. Payment. The buyer goes to the payment system page and pays for the order.
  5. Reservation. The paid product is reserved, and the number of available units decreases.

Tilda integrates natively with popular payment providers. In Russian practice these are YooKassa, Robokassa, Tinkoff, and others, while the help center mentions Stripe, PayPal, and Verifone; there is a pay-on-delivery option and promo codes (help.tilda.cc). When payment starts, the product is reserved in the catalog, and auto-release of the reservation is configurable in a range from 15 minutes to 7 days (help.tilda.cc).

Whom Tilda fits and whom it does not

We say this honestly up front, so you do not pay for a platform that will not suit you. Tilda is a no-code builder with a store, not a full CMS at the level of Bitrix or Shopify. E-commerce functions exist but are shallow: a basic catalog, cart, checkout, and minimal stock tracking (ecomm.design).

Tilda fits if you have:

  • a small assortment and a first or second store;
  • a need for a fast selling start and a limited budget;
  • no exchange with 1C and no real-time inventory tracking;
  • a wish to run the catalog yourself, without a developer.

Tilda will be too small if you need a heavy catalog with deep filtering, exchange with 1C, thousands of SKUs, and high load. That is already a store on Bitrix, a larger and heavier solution.

SEO of an online store on Tilda

A store on Tilda can and should be prepared for search. We set human-readable page addresses, write unique titles and descriptions per category and card, build a sitemap, and watch loading speed. Tilda lets you manage these settings, so the store reaches search no worse than on a CMS, provided the basic optimization is done carefully.

Separately, we connect your own domain and remove the builder mention, so the store looks like an independent brand rather than a template site. This affects both buyer trust and how search engines perceive the store.

How much a store on Tilda costs and the timeline

The cost is made up of two parts: the Tilda subscription and the turnkey studio work. You pay the subscription to the platform and the development to us.

Official Tilda pricing: the Personal plan costs 500 RUB/month when paid yearly or 750 RUB/month monthly, the Business plan costs 1000 RUB/month when paid yearly or 1250 RUB/month monthly. For a store you take Business.

Turnkey store development on the Russian market costs from 60,000 RUB with a freelancer, from 80,000 RUB in a regional studio, and from 100,000 RUB in a Moscow or Saint Petersburg studio. We work from your assortment and set of integrations, so we lock the exact estimate after a short review of the task. Launch takes from 2 weeks depending on catalog size and the number of payment systems.

When you outgrow Tilda

Tilda is designed for a small, fast store, and that is its strength, not a flaw. But business grows, and at some point the platform becomes too small. Signs that it is time to think about moving:

  • the catalog has grown to thousands of positions and become hard to manage;
  • you need exchange with 1C and real-time inventory;
  • complex filtering and personal pricing have appeared;
  • load has grown, and performance and flexibility now matter more.

In that case we move the store to a more powerful platform without losing sales. That is already an online store on Bitrix: a large catalog, integration with 1C, thousands of SKUs, and high load. Tilda remains the fast start, and Bitrix becomes the next step once the store has genuinely grown.

How development goes

We run the work in clear stages, so you see progress and timelines.

  1. Task review. We clarify the assortment, payment and delivery methods, and gather the catalog structure.
  2. Catalog and cards. We set up products, categories, and cards, and configure the cart.
  3. Payment and delivery. We connect payment systems and delivery methods, and check the checkout.
  4. Design and SEO. We style the store to your brand and set up addresses, meta tags, and the sitemap.
  5. Launch. We connect the domain, test payment on a real order, and open sales.
