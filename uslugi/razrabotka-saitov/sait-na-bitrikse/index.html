Turnkey Website Development on 1C-Bitrix

Here we build a site on the "1C-Bitrix: Site Management" engine, a paid Russian CMS for external sites, catalogs and portals. Let us first separate two products that people often confuse because of the shared brand. "1C-Bitrix" is a site management system, while "Bitrix24" is a separate product, a CRM and corporate portal for a company's internal work. If you specifically need a CRM, head to the Bitrix24 implementation page. If you need a site on the Bitrix engine, you are on the right page. We build such sites turnkey within the website development service: design, front-end, 1C integration and handover with support.

Why businesses choose a 1C-Bitrix website

A 1C-Bitrix site is a site on the commercial "1C-Bitrix: Site Management" CMS, a system with a legal Russian license, ready-made 1C integration and built-in speed and protection mechanisms. Businesses choose it when a project outgrows a website builder and needs complex logic. Per market data, 1C-Bitrix leads among online-store CMS platforms in Russia with a share of about 60.8% in the e-commerce segment.

Three reasons businesses settle on this engine:

  • 1C exchange out of the box. Products, prices, stock and orders sync with "1C:Enterprise" over a standard protocol, without homegrown workarounds.
  • Russian license and support. A legal product with vendor updates and technical support, which matters for companies with import-independence requirements.
  • Room to grow. One engine handles a corporate site, a large catalog and an internal portal alike, so the project does not need a rewrite as it scales.

1C-Bitrix (site CMS) and Bitrix24 (CRM) are different products

This is the most common mix-up, and it is worth clearing up before the start. There is one brand but two products, and they solve different tasks. Confusing them is costly: a site and a CRM need different licenses, teams and timelines.

Parameter1C-Bitrix: Site ManagementBitrix24

A simple rule: everything your client sees in a browser is a 1C-Bitrix site. Everything your managers work in inside the company is Bitrix24. We handle the setup and implementation of the second product separately on the Bitrix24 implementation page, and this page is only about the site.

What kinds of sites we build on 1C-Bitrix

The engine is justified on projects with complex structure and integrations. The main types we develop:

  • Corporate site. A multi-page company site with services, a solutions catalog, a blog and forms. More on the format on the corporate site page.
  • Site with a catalog. A showcase with products, filters, specifications and 1C sync, but without a full online-store cart.
  • Corporate portal or B2B account area. Personal accounts, closed sections, roles and complex access rights.

If the task is simpler, for example a landing page or a business-card site without 1C and a catalog, the Bitrix engine is overkill. In such cases we honestly suggest a lighter platform, for example a site on Tilda, and we do not sell a heavy license where it is not needed.

Integrating the site with 1C:Enterprise

The main reason to choose this engine is seamless exchange with the accounting system. The site and "1C:Enterprise" exchange data over the CommerceML standard in XML format through a native exchange endpoint. The catalog, prices and stock flow from 1C to the site, while placed orders go from the site back to 1C. The exchange is two-way and runs on a schedule, so prices and availability on the site stay current without manual entry.

This removes double work and manual-transfer errors: the manager keeps the product list in the familiar 1C, and the site picks up changes on its own. It is precisely for this integration that the Bitrix engine is most often chosen over lighter platforms.

SEO, speed and security on Bitrix

The engine covers the technical basics for search and load without third-party plugins. For SEO out of the box you get human-readable URLs for all content types, control of title and description at the section and element level, auto-generation of sitemap.xml and robots.txt, canonical and redirect setup.

Speed is handled by composite mode: the engine serves the page as ready static HTML from cache in fractions of a second, while dynamic blocks such as the cart and personal account load with a separate request. A fast page response is both a ranking factor and a convenience for the visitor.

Security on the commercial editions is covered by proactive protection, a built-in web firewall. It filters SQL injection and cross-site scripting, guards against password brute-forcing and keeps an attack log. For a site tied to an accounting system and orders, this lowers the risk of leaks and downtime.

How much a 1C-Bitrix site costs

The cost is made up of four parts: the CMS license, design and development, integrations with 1C and other systems, and post-launch support. So the final estimate depends on the site type and the depth of integrations.

Market benchmarks for 2025-2026:

  • A corporate site usually runs 200,000 – 900,000 RUB.
  • A site with a catalog ranges from 400,000 RUB to 1.5 million RUB depending on the size of the product list and filters.
  • A portal or B2B platform costs 1.5 – 5 million RUB because of the complex logic and roles.

We lock the exact estimate after a short brief, once the site type, catalog size and integration list are clear. We do not quote a price blindly and do not inflate it with editions and hours the project does not need.

How development goes

We run development in steps so you see the result at every stage, not just at the end.

  1. Brief and prototype. We gather requirements, structure and scenarios, and agree on a prototype of the key pages.
  2. Design. We draw mockups for your brand and approve the visuals before front-end.
  3. Development on Bitrix. We build the template, set up sections, the catalog and forms on the engine.
  4. 1C integration. We set up CommerceML exchange and verify the export of products, prices and orders.
  5. Launch and support. We move to the production server, configure security and hand over with support.

Timeline benchmarks are as follows: a corporate site takes 3 – 4 weeks, a site with a catalog 6 – 10 weeks, and a large portal starts from 3 – 4 months.
