Real Estate Agency CRM: Properties, Matching and Auto-Posting to Portals in One System

A realtor works not with an abstract deal, but with a bundle of client, property, showing and portal listing. A regular CRM tracks the lead and the deal, but does not store a property database, cannot match options to a request and does not post them to Cian, Avito and Domclick. We select and set up CRM specifically for agency processes, so that properties, matching, pipeline and publishing live in one system instead of Excel and a dozen tabs. This is part of our CRM implementation practice: first we study your processes, then we configure the system around them.

Why a real estate agency needs a CRM

A real estate CRM is a system that ties together the four entities of the realtor business: client, property, deal and portal listing. This exact bundle is what sets it apart from a universal CRM. A regular system moves a lead through the pipeline and stores contacts, but it has no property card with photos, layout, price and status, just as it has no matching of options to a buyer's request.

In practice this means that in an industry CRM the same property is visible in the database, linked to the right clients through matching, moves through its own deal pipeline and goes out as a listing to portals all at once. The manager no longer keeps these links in their head or moves data by hand between systems.

What a realtor loses without a CRM

While the property and client base lives in spreadsheets and messengers, an agency loses money at three points. Requests from portals and calls get forgotten or handled late, and the client leaves for whoever answered first. The property base goes stale: the price changed, the property went on reserve, but the listing still hangs and generates empty calls. Publishing eats up hours, because every property has to be entered manually into Avito, Cian, Domclick and Yandex.Realty one by one.

Auto-posting through a single XML feed removes exactly the third loss. By the estimate of industry integrators, it saves around 40–60 minutes per property versus manual duplication across all portals. On a base of a hundred active properties that is dozens of hours a month that go into copy-paste instead of showings and deals.

Real estate CRM features: properties, matching, unit grid, feed

What sets an industry CRM apart from a universal one is the feature set built for real estate work. The baseline profile is this:

  • Property database. The card stores photos, layouts, address, area, floor, price, commission, status (available, reserved, sold) and price change history.
  • Matching by request. The system itself suggests properties for the client's parameters and records what was shown and to whom.
  • New-build unit grid. A visual grid of apartments by building and floor with statuses for work with developers.
  • Pipelines by deal type. Separate pipelines for purchase, sale, new builds, resale and commercial, instead of one for everything.
  • Auto-posting. A single feed publishes properties to portals and updates them when the price or status changes.
  • Request intake from portals. Listing inquiries and calls land in the CRM as leads and are not lost.

How the deal pipeline works in real estate

A deal at an agency is longer than in retail and runs through eight predictable stages. It is convenient to track them in a separate pipeline, to see exactly where clients get stuck.

The client journey looks like this: first contact, qualification, property matching, showing, negotiation, deposit, deal, close. For purchase, sale, new builds and resale the stage logic differs, so in the CRM you set up several pipelines instead of trying to fit everything into one. Then the manager sees the conversion of each stage and understands whether clients are lost at matching, at showings or already at negotiation.

Which CRM suits a real estate agency

The choice comes down to a fork: a dedicated real estate CRM or a universal platform with an industry module. Dedicated solutions have the property database and matching built in out of the box. Universal platforms like Bitrix24 and amoCRM are strong in analytics, telephony and tasks, and add property work through industry add-ons.

Integration with portals: Cian, Avito, Domclick, Yandex.Realty

Auto-posting rests on a single XML feed. You enter a property once in the CRM, and the system builds the feed and publishes the listing to the connected portals: Cian, Avito, Domclick, Yandex.Realty and others. The exact set depends on the region and often includes Yula and Mail.ru Realty. When the price or status of a property changes, the listings update without manual edits on each site.

The link works in the other direction too. Listing messages and calls from portals are relayed into the CRM as requests and reach the responsible manager. That way an inquiry from Avito or Cian is not lost in personal chats, but immediately becomes a lead with history and a reminder.

What we set up during implementation

Implementation is not installing a box, but tuning the system to how your agency actually works. We break down the processes and assemble a configuration around them.

The baseline scope of work is this: a property database with the needed fields and statuses, pipelines for your deal types, client cards and matching rules, integration with portals through the feed, request intake from calls and listings, access rights for realtors and managers, reports for the owner on properties, deals and manager performance. Separately we migrate the current property and client base from Excel or an old system, so you do not start from a blank sheet.
