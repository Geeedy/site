Beauty Salon CRM: We Select and Configure the System for Your Beauty Business

Appointments get lost in chat, staff either sit idle or work back to back, and the client who came once never returned. That is how a salon runs without a single system of record. A beauty salon CRM brings online booking, the staff schedule and the client base into one place, where every free minute in the chair and every client due for a return visit is visible. We select and configure such a system for a specific salon honestly: in one case it is an industry booking service, in another a universal CRM, and often a combination of the two. This is part of our approach to CRM implementation: we count the process before and after, rather than selling a box.

Pick a system for your salon Ask a question

Why a beauty salon needs CRM and online booking

A beauty salon CRM is a system that runs online booking, the schedule of staff and rooms, a card for every client with visit history, and reminds about the appointment automatically. Put simply, instead of a paper journal and the receptionist's notes, one base appears that shows who is booked with whom and when, who has not come in for a while, and how much each specialist earned. The client books at any hour on their own, the receptionist does not call to confirm, and the owner sees chair utilization and revenue in real numbers rather than by feel.

For a small studio or a single specialist this is often enough in a basic form, while a salon chain already calls for tighter automation. If your task is broader than beauty specifics and comes down to the size of the business, look at the separate breakdown of CRM for small business, where the segmentation goes by scale rather than by industry.

What a salon loses without tracking appointments and the client base

While appointments live in the receptionist's head and messenger chats, the losses are invisible, yet they happen every day. A client booked and did not show up, the slot in the schedule stayed empty, the specialist got idle time instead of income. By industry estimates the average no-show rate in beauty is 15–30% of appointments, and in Russia, per NAFI and Profi.ru, it is 18–20%, meaning every sixth client. Profit lost to such no-shows reaches up to 22% of a salon's monthly revenue.

At the same time, online booking is still far from universal: only about 54% of beauty businesses in Russia let the client book on their own, even though demand for self-booking is growing. The number of online appointments with specialists rose 71% over 2024, and self-booking is used by more than 150,000 specialists and salons. A salon without a system loses twice: on no-shows and on clients who moved to those who are easier to book with.

What features a beauty salon CRM needs

The beauty specific is that you sell not a product but the specialist's time in the chair. That is why the feature set differs from an ordinary CRM.

  • Online booking 24/7. The client picks the service, specialist and free time themselves, on the website, in social media or via a widget, without calling the receptionist.
  • Schedule of staff and rooms. Each specialist's timetable, occupancy of chairs and rooms, so there are no clashes or empty slots.
  • Client card and visit history. What was done, with which specialist, which materials, what preferences and contraindications, when a repeat visit is due.
  • Auto-reminders. A message in WhatsApp or Telegram a day and an hour before the visit, so the client does not forget and shows up.
  • Loyalty program. Bonuses, discounts for regulars, packages and promotions that bring the client back instead of letting them go to a competitor.
  • Supplies inventory. Tracking materials by service, writing off dye and cosmetics, stock control, so the specialist is not left without what they need in the middle of the day.

Which integrations we connect for a salon

A booking system is valuable when it is linked with the salon's other tools. We set up integrations for beauty processes.

  • Online cash register per 54-FZ. The receipt is issued automatically when a service is paid, sales and materials are accounted for by law.
  • Acquiring and online payment. Prepayment for a visit and selling packages online, which also cuts no-shows.
  • WhatsApp and Telegram. Auto-reminders, booking confirmations and win-back campaigns in the channels where the client actually reads.
  • Telephony. A call is tied to the client card, the receptionist sees who is calling and with what history.
  • Booking widget on the website and social media. A booking button right on the website, in Instagram, VKontakte and on maps, without the client leaving their usual place.

What we configure during implementation

Implementation is not installing a program, it is moving the salon's work into the system without losing data and without receptionists panicking.

  1. Client base migration. We pull the history from the old program, Excel or a notebook and move it into client cards with visits and contacts.
  2. Schedule of services and staff. We set up the price list, procedure durations, each specialist's timetable, rooms and combined services.
  3. Reminder scenarios. We configure when and in which channel a reminder, confirmation and win-back message go out.
  4. Staff payroll. We set up calculation by percentages, salaries and services, so payouts are counted from actual visits rather than by hand.
  5. Receptionist training. We show the team how to manage bookings, work with the client card and not fear the system, so nothing stalls on the very first day.

The benchmark for the decision is simple: implementation should cost less than the salon loses on no-shows and departed clients over the same period.

How implementation goes: stages and timelines

Timelines depend on whether we set up just online booking or a bundle of a service, CRM, telephony and loyalty.

  1. Process audit. We look at how the salon handles booking and the base now, where clients and money leak.
  2. System selection. We choose an industry service, a universal CRM or their combination for your tasks and budget.
  3. Setup and migration. We enter the price list and schedule, migrate the base, connect the cash register and reminders.
  4. Training and launch. We train receptionists and staff, launch booking and support the first days.

Basic online booking implementation takes 1–2 weeks (7–14 business days), while a full project with an industry service, CRM, telephony and loyalty bundle runs from 4 to 8 weeks.
