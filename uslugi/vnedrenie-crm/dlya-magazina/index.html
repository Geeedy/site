CRM for a retail store: one customer base, loyalty and repeat sales

In most retail stores the customer base is smeared across the register, a notebook and the salesperson's memory. Receipts are rung up anonymously, no one counts repeat sales, and campaigns go out "to everyone" or never go out at all. CRM for retail links every receipt to a customer card, shows who has not come back for a while, and launches repeat sales automatically. We are an independent integrator: we select a system to fit your store format and set it up around real processes rather than selling a boxed product. This is part of our CRM implementation service, where the effect is measured before and after.

Why a store needs CRM

CRM for a store is a system that tracks the customer, not the product and not the receipt. Let us break down three tools that retail often confuses. An online cash register records the fact of a sale and sends the data to the tax office. Inventory software counts stock and warehouse movements. CRM stores the customer: who bought, what, how often and for how much, so you can bring them back for a repeat purchase. The register counts receipts, inventory software counts boxes, CRM counts people and their returns to the store. Full retail operations rest on all three, but it is CRM that turns one-off receipts into regular customers.

What a store loses without CRM

Without CRM a store loses money quietly, because the losses do not show up in the revenue report. An anonymous receipt is tied to no one, so there is nothing to bring that person back with. "Dormant" customers who stopped coming do not land on any list, and they are simply forgotten. Every sale is counted as one-off, even though acquiring a new customer costs several times more than retaining a familiar one. Campaigns without segmentation fly out to everyone the same way, annoy people and burn out the base. In the end the budget goes to new traffic, while your own audience, which has already bought and trusts the store, sits idle.

Retail CRM functions: base, loyalty, RFM, campaigns

What matters to a retail CRM is not B2B "deals" but working with the flow of customers. The key functions are these:

  • A single customer profile covering phone, email, WhatsApp, history of receipts, returns, visits and reactions to campaigns in one card.
  • Loyalty program with points, tiers, personal discounts and accrual rules tied to purchases.
  • RFM segments with automatic splitting of the base by recency, frequency and amount of purchases.
  • Trigger scenarios including a welcome for a new customer, a reminder to buy more, reactivation of the "dormant", a return after the first purchase.
  • Omnichannel campaigns by SMS, email and WhatsApp from one window to the segment you need.
  • Repeat-sales and LTV analytics covering average check, purchase frequency, churn and customer lifetime value.

It is the combination of "one base + trigger communications + segment analytics" that sets retail CRM apart from a simple contact list in cash-register software.

How repeat sales and RFM segmentation work

RFM segmentation is a way to break the base into clear groups along three axes. Recency shows how long ago a person bought, Frequency how often, Monetary for how much. Each customer gets a score on these axes, and the system assembles segments from them on its own: VIP, loyal, customers at risk of leaving and "dormant". Then its own communication kicks in for each segment. A newcomer gets a welcome chain, a regular gets a personal offer for a favorite category, a "dormant" one gets reactivation with an incentive to return.

A store calculates customer lifetime value as the average check multiplied by purchase frequency and by customer lifespan (retailcrm.ru, storeradar.com). Separately the system tracks the share of repeat purchases and churn. To understand the real effect of loyalty rather than an imaginary one, the measurement is run against a control group without campaigns and compared with it for the gain in repeat sales. That shows exactly what the program brought in, rather than the season or general foot traffic.

Integrations: 54-FZ register, warehouse, marketplaces

The value of retail CRM shows itself in the connections. The main one is linking receipts from the online cash register to the customer card. ATOL and Evotor registers pass sale data under 54-FZ, and CRM matches the receipt to the customer by phone or loyalty card. After that the store sees purchase frequency, average check and the path to a repeat visit for each person.

The second loop is product and stock. MoySklad or 1C keep stock up to date, and CRM relies on them for campaigns and recommendations. A ready MoySklad-to-Bitrix24 bundle costs about 29,000 RUB one-time. The third loop is marketplaces. MoySklad sends stock and orders to Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market, and a package connection of all three platforms costs from 3,500 RUB/mo. Note that from 23.03.2026, for a single connector to Ozon or Wildberries, the MoySklad subscription must include 4 online-commerce options. Repeat-sales data from these loops is convenient to pull together into end-to-end analytics, so you can see the payback of every campaign and channel.

What we set up during implementation

Turnkey implementation is not installing software but tuning it to your store. We do the following:

  • A customer card with a history of receipts, returns and touches, linked to the register and loyalty cards.
  • A loyalty program with points, tiers and accrual rules to fit your margin.
  • RFM segments that update automatically as new purchases come in.
  • Trigger campaigns for welcome, repeat purchase and reactivation of the "dormant".
  • Salesperson permissions, so each one sees only their own and cannot export the base.
  • Owner reports on repeat sales, average check, churn and the loyalty effect.

We drive the setup from the store's processes rather than from checkboxes in the interface, so the system solves concrete retail tasks from day one.

How implementation goes and why us

Implementation runs through clear stages. First an audit of processes and data: how sales are set up, what is already in the register and spreadsheets. Then selecting a system for your format and migrating the customer base. Next, setting up the card, loyalty, RFM segments and trigger campaigns, connecting the register, warehouse and marketplaces. Finally training the salespeople and going live, and after that support and adjustment of scenarios based on the first results. A basic project for a single outlet takes weeks, for a chain longer because of the number of integrations.
