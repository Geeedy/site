Generative AI for Business: We Build Assistants, Copilots, and Content Generation on LLM

Generative AI has stopped being a news topic and become a working tool. A model writes a reply to a customer, drafts a document, checks code, and finds the right item in a chat archive in seconds. We build such solutions for a company's specific processes instead of selling a box. This is part of our approach to AI deployment for business tasks: we start with a narrow task, measure the effect, and pick a model that matches your data requirements.

What generative AI is in plain words

Generative AI is a technology that creates new content (text, code, images) from a natural-language request, drawing on large language models. Unlike analytical AI, which classifies data and builds forecasts, generative AI produces a result: an assistant writes a reply to a customer, a copilot puts together a contract draft, a model formulates a product description. You state the task in words, and the system returns finished text or code.

How generative AI differs from ordinary AI

The term "artificial intelligence" covers different technologies, and the confusion costs money when you choose a solution. Analytical AI answers the question "what will happen" based on your numbers, while generative AI creates new material on request. The difference shows in the table.

TraitAnalytical AIGenerative AI
What it doesClassifies, forecastsCreates new content
Input and outputData turns into a label or numberA request turns into text, code, an image
Example taskScoring, antifraud, demand forecastAssistant, copilot, text generation

The two technologies often work in tandem: the forecast suggests what will happen, and the generative layer drafts an email or report about it.

How generative AI works: models, transformers, training

At the core of the solution is a large language model (LLM) trained on huge text corpora. Most modern models rest on the transformer architecture, which captures links between words and holds the context of a request. The scheme is simple: you give a prompt and context, the transformer-based model predicts the answer word by word, and a person checks the result before it goes into action.

Answer quality depends on two things: the data the model works with and the tuning for the task. That is why the same model can be brought to a precise working tool or produce pretty but useless text. We cover quality control below, in the section on the human role and hallucinations.

Where generative AI brings business value

Value appears where an employee spends time on repetitive work with text or search. The main uses that pay off in small and medium business:

  • We answer customers. An assistant handles routine requests on the website and in messengers, and passes complex ones to a person.
  • We prepare documents. A copilot drafts emails, proposals, and contracts using the company template.
  • We write and check code. A developer gets hints and starter code right in the editor.
  • We search internal data. An employee asks in plain language and gets an answer with a link to the document. This is a separate service, search across internal documents (RAG).
  • We generate marketing content. Descriptions, posts, and newsletters in the brand's tone.

We spun off some of these scenarios into separate services because each has its own specifics. Dialogue scenarios on the website and in messengers are handled by AI chatbots, while multi-step tasks that call external systems are taken on by AI agents for multi-step tasks. The scale of adoption is backed by research: McKinsey attributes about 75% of generative AI's potential value to four functions (customer operations, marketing and sales, software engineering, R&D) McKinsey, The economic potential of generative AI.

What we build on generative AI

We assemble three types of solutions, and each is built for the client's processes rather than sold as a ready-made platform.

  • Assistants. They answer customers and staff in chat, on the website, and in messengers, offload the first support line, and work around the clock.
  • Copilots. They prompt inside work tools and generate drafts of emails, documents, and code, so an employee edits a ready draft instead of writing from scratch.
  • Content generation. Texts, descriptions, posts, and newsletters in your brand's template and tone, with a check before publication.

The solution is built for your processes, data, and security requirements. One business needs an assistant in the cloud, another a copilot in a closed environment with no data leaving it. We match the configuration to the task instead of forcing the task into one product.

How development and deployment go

Development runs step by step, and at each one you can see whether the solution pays off.

  1. We review the task and data. We pick a scenario with a clear cost of error and check what data and access are enough.
  2. We choose a model and test it on a pilot. We match an LLM to the data requirements and build a working prototype.
  3. We assemble the solution and integrate it. We embed it into your channels and systems: website, messengers, CRM, work tools.
  4. We test quality and adjust prompts. We run it on real examples and refine wording and limits.
  5. We launch and support it. We watch answer quality and retrain it for changes in your processes.

The human role, hallucinations, and rules of use

Generative AI has a trait worth knowing before deployment. A model can produce plausible but wrong answers, and this is called hallucination. It does not "know" a fact but predicts text that looks true, so in critical scenarios a person checks the answer.

We reduce the risk at the architecture level: we limit topics, steer the model toward verified sources instead of free invention, and build rules of use into the workflows. So the team works with the tool confidently, we separately run team training on working with AI: where an answer can be relied on and where a human check is needed.
